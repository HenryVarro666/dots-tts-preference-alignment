# Preference Alignment for Continuous-Latent Flow-Matching TTS: An Open DPO Recipe and a Controlled Study of Reward Hacking

*Alternative titles considered (for author decision): "DPO without Discrete Audio Tokens: Open Preference Optimization over Flow-Matching Velocity Loss for Continuous-Latent Autoregressive TTS"; "FlowAlign: Reproducible Multi-Objective DPO for Continuous-Latent TTS with Streaming Transfer and Reward-Hacking Diagnosis."*

## Abstract

Continuous-latent autoregressive (AR) TTS — exemplified by dots.tts, which generates audio through a flow-matching DiT head over a continuous AudioVAE with **no discrete audio tokens** — is robust, but its preference alignment is unsolved: standard Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) requires token log-probabilities that continuous-latent systems do not have. We study the *natural* adaptation, **DPO-FM**, which (following Diffusion-DPO) substitutes dots.tts's flow-matching velocity-MSE loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ as an implicit log-likelihood surrogate, using four shared-RNG forward passes to keep the policy/reference comparison valid. Our central result is cautionary and mechanistic: a direct, training-free probe over the **full $N=188$ preference set** shows this surrogate is **misspecified** for continuous-latent TTS. On average $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ shows **no systematic preference** for the perceptually preferred candidate (its denoised margin is positive on only 44% of pairs; mean winner win-rate 0.47, indistinguishable from a 0.5 coin-flip), **yet** it is significantly **anti-correlated** with the perceptual reward gap (Pearson $\rho=-0.411$, $p<10^{-7}$; Spearman $-0.187$, $p\approx10^{-2}$). These two facts are complementary: the absent average preference explains why DPO-FM under-fits (its reward margin stalls near zero), while the robust anti-correlation means that on exactly the pairs with the *largest* quality gap the surrogate points the *wrong* way. Mechanistically, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ tracks typicality under the model's own flow head rather than perceptual quality, so the highest-reward (often highest-guidance) candidates are the lowest-likelihood ones; this jointly explains the objective's under-fitting and over-optimization and warns that discrete-token preference recipes do not transplant to continuous-latent TTS unexamined. We test and exclude a candidate-generation (guidance) confound — winner and loser generation settings are statistically indistinguishable (Pearson $|\rho|\le 0.12$) — so the anti-correlation is intrinsic to $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$. We also report an honest revision: an earlier $n=24$ pilot over-stated an *average* winner-dispreference (apparent win-rate 0.33) that regressed to chance at full scale, whereas the anti-correlation persisted and *gained* significance. The two arms that avoid the win/lose $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ contrast — reward-free self-correction (**open-SOAR**, an open approximation of dots.tts's unpublished SOAR) and SFT-on-winners — are *not* subject to this misspecification and remain viable directions. Alongside, we release multi-objective preference-pair construction (WER + speaker similarity + UTMOS) and *controlled* evidence of reward hacking: WER-only alignment attains the lowest transcription error but degrades naturalness (UTMOS $\downarrow$ __) and expressiveness (EmergentTTS win-rate $\downarrow$ __), while multi-objective rewards recover both; and we verify that offline alignment gains transfer to one-token-one-audio (1T1A) streaming with no measurable increase in first-chunk latency. All code, preference-pair construction, the diagnostic probe, and evaluation pipelines are released under Apache-2.0.

## 1. Introduction

Speech synthesis has long been dominated by two paradigms: end-to-end acoustic models (e.g., FastSpeech2, VITS) and, more recently, discrete codec language models that autoregressively generate quantized audio tokens (VALL-E, Wang et al., 2023; SoundStorm, Borsos et al., 2023). A third paradigm is now emerging: *continuous-latent* AR TTS, which retains the expressive power of autoregressive generation while bypassing the quantization bottleneck entirely. dots.tts (rednote-hilab & SJTU X-LANCE, 2026) is the clearest embodiment of this approach: a Qwen2.5-1.5B language-model backbone emits patch-level hidden states that condition a DiT flow-matching head, which denoises continuous AudioVAE latents at 48 kHz — no discrete audio token appears anywhere in the pipeline. On robustness benchmarks (seed-tts-eval, Anastassiou et al., 2024), dots.tts matches or outperforms codec-LM systems at higher fidelity, and its SOAR post-training further strengthens robustness through an unpublished self-correction procedure.

Despite these advances, continuous-latent TTS faces two alignment challenges that the literature has not addressed. **First**, DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) has become the dominant alignment tool for language models, but it relies on token-level sequence log-probabilities — a quantity that is structurally unavailable in systems whose acoustic output is sampled from a continuous stochastic ODE. To our knowledge, no published recipe instantiates DPO on a continuous-latent AR TTS model. **Second**, *reward hacking* — a model optimized for a single automatic metric (e.g., word error rate, WER) attaining that metric at the cost of naturalness, speaker fidelity, or expressiveness — is widely acknowledged in TTS practitioner discourse but has not been demonstrated in a controlled, reproducible setup with held-out naturalness measures.

Existing TTS post-training efforts close neither gap. REINFORCE-based methods for codec-LM TTS (Seed-TTS, Anastassiou et al., 2024) operate only in discrete token spaces; DPO/GSPO adaptations for TTS (Qwen3-TTS) likewise assume exact token log-probabilities. For continuous-latent architectures, the closest precedent is Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2023), which shows a diffusion model's training loss is a valid log-likelihood surrogate — but this insight has not been adapted to the patch-AR setting of flow-matching TTS with a separate language-model backbone. On the self-correction side, dots.tts's SOAR *weights* are released, yet the training procedure is entirely undocumented, making SOAR non-reproducible and impossible to isolate as an experimental variable.

**In one sentence:** we develop the *natural* adaptation of DPO to continuous-latent flow-matching AR TTS (instantiated on dots.tts), then show — via a direct diagnostic probe — that this adaptation is *misspecified*, because its flow-matching implicit-reward surrogate anti-correlates with perceptual reward; the paper is therefore a cautionary, mechanistic study of why porting DPO/Diffusion-DPO to continuous-latent TTS fails, what that implies, and which alignment routes escape the failure.

We make five contributions; together they reframe the work from a "working recipe" into a cautionary, mechanistic study of why the natural DPO-FM adaptation is misspecified for continuous-latent TTS.

1. **DPO-FM: the natural DPO adaptation for continuous-latent flow-matching, and a test of its premise.** Adapting Diffusion-DPO, we derive a DPO objective for continuous-latent AR TTS that substitutes the flow-matching velocity-MSE loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ as an implicit log-likelihood surrogate, using exactly four forward passes per pair — policy/reference × winner/loser — that share a single noise/timestep draw $(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$ to keep comparisons valid, with an anchor SFT term on winners to prevent drift of the text-conditioning and end-of-sequence heads. This is the first public preference-alignment implementation for any continuous-latent flow-matching TTS architecture; rather than presenting it as a finished recipe, we subject its load-bearing premise — that $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ tracks perceptual reward — to a direct empirical test.

2. **The FM-loss implicit reward is misspecified ($\rho=-0.41$ anti-correlation, $N=188$): the central, cautionary finding.** A training-free probe (`alignment/probe_fm_reward.py`) over the **full set of $N=188$ preference pairs** shows $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ fails as an implicit reward in two distinct senses. **(i) No systematic preference on average:** the denoised FM margin is positive on only **82/188 (44%)** of pairs (mean **$-0.0071$**, mean winner win-rate **0.47**) — indistinguishable from a 0.5 coin-flip — directly explaining why DPO-FM's reward margin stalls near zero (under-fitting). **(ii) Robust significant anti-correlation:** the FM margin is significantly *anti*-correlated with the perceptual reward gap (**Pearson $-0.411$, $p<10^{-7}$; Spearman $-0.187$, $p\approx10^{-2}$**; critical $|r|\approx0.143$ at $N=188$), so on the pairs with the *largest* quality gap the surrogate prefers the *loser*. Because the surrogate measures typicality under the model's own flow head rather than perceptual quality, the highest-reward (often highest-guidance) candidates are the lowest-likelihood ones; this mechanistically explains DPO-FM's instability (§6.2) and warns that discrete-token / image-diffusion preference recipes do not transplant to continuous-latent TTS unexamined. Two safeguards frame the finding. First, an honest revision: an earlier $n=24$ pilot appeared stronger (positive on 4/24, win-rate 0.33, Pearson $-0.496$), but at full scale the apparent *average* winner-dispreference regressed to chance while the *anti-correlation* persisted and the Spearman coefficient moved from non-significant (at $n=24$) to significant (at $N=188$). Second, a confound exclusion: a metadata probe (`candidates/inspect_guidance_confound.py`, joining all 188 pairs to their generation settings) finds winner/loser settings statistically indistinguishable (Pearson $|\rho|\le 0.12$ between the reward gap and each of guidance scale, num-steps, and speaker scale), so the anti-correlation is **intrinsic** to $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ rather than a candidate-generation artifact.

3. **open-SOAR and SFT: alignment routes that bypass the misspecified premise.** We release **open-SOAR**, a reward-free open approximation of dots.tts's undisclosed SOAR self-correction whose preference is defined by *trajectory cleanliness* (clean target trajectory vs. perturbed-rollout velocity field, clean target retained) rather than an external reward. Together with SFT-on-winners — which performs no win/lose $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ contrast at all — these are exactly the arms whose preference signal does *not* pass through the anti-correlated FM-loss contrast, and thus the constructive directions our diagnosis points toward.

4. **Controlled reproduction of reward hacking (supporting negative result).** We deliberately train a WER-only arm (`dpo_weronly`): it attains the lowest WER of all treatments (__) but degrades naturalness (UTMOS $\downarrow$ __, 95% CI [__, __]) and expressiveness (EmergentTTS win-rate $\downarrow$ __), whereas a multi-objective reward (`dpo_multiobj`: WER + speaker cosine + UTMOS, $z$-normalized per prompt pool, weighted 0.4/0.3/0.3) recovers both — to our knowledge the first published reproducible evidence of the reward-hacking trade-off in TTS alignment.

5. **Streaming-first evaluation.** We verify that gains persist under 1T1A streaming decoding — the production-serving mode — with no statistically significant increase in time-to-first-audio (TTFA, i.e., first-chunk latency), closing the gap between offline alignment research and latency-constrained deployment.

Experiments use a held-out 100-utterance LJSpeech subset (zero overlap with the 188 preference pairs), seed-tts-eval–style metrics (WER via Whisper-large-v3, speaker similarity via WavLM-SV, UTMOS, EmergentTTS-Eval LLM-judge win-rate), and streaming metrics. Our pre-registered gate — hard-case WER reduction relative to `base` with SIM degradation $\leq 0.01$ and no UTMOS decline — is __ by `dpo_multiobj`. Given the intentionally small scale, all deltas carry bootstrap 95% CIs, and we caution against reading absolute scores as cross-paper SOTA. All code, recipes, and evaluation tooling are released under Apache-2.0.

## 2. Related Work

### 2.1 Preference Alignment: RLHF, DPO, and Diffusion-DPO

RLHF (Christiano et al., 2017; Ouyang et al., 2022) established the paradigm of training a reward model on preferences and optimizing the policy via PPO — powerful but expensive and prone to reward over-optimization, a pathology we exploit as a controlled condition in §5.3. DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) removes the explicit reward model by reparameterizing the RL objective as binary cross-entropy over the policy's log-likelihood ratio between preferred and dispreferred completions. Variants address specific failure modes — IPO (Azar et al., 2024) adds identity regularization; GSPO (Qwen3-TTS) uses group-relative normalization for candidates sampled from the same prompt. **All standard DPO formulations assume tractable per-sequence log-likelihoods**, an assumption that fails for continuous-latent generative models with no discrete tokens and no closed-form $\log\pi(y\mid x)$.

Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2023) solves the analogous problem for image diffusion: since the denoising loss is a variational bound on $-\log p$, it serves as a principled log-likelihood surrogate, and a *shared* noise sample and timestep across the four forward passes ensure comparability. We adopt both the surrogate-loss insight and the shared-RNG constraint, transplanting them from image diffusion to **flow-matching continuous-latent AR speech synthesis**. Three non-trivial adaptations are required: the reference is a frozen AR-model copy rather than a fixed noise prior; the surrogate is velocity-MSE from a DiT head over AudioVAE latents rather than pixel-space denoising loss; and an anchor SFT term on winners prevents drift in the LLM backbone's text conditioning — a component absent in image alignment, where no language model is involved. Critically, all three adaptations *preserve* Diffusion-DPO's load-bearing premise — that the surrogate loss is a likelihood proxy aligned with perceptual *quality* — yet none of them *guarantees* it. In image diffusion the denoising loss is computed in the same pixel space a human perceives, so lower loss plausibly means a better picture; in continuous-latent TTS the surrogate instead lives in an AudioVAE latent decoded by a separate flow head, so whether $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ aligns with perceptual reward is an empirical question rather than an inherited guarantee. This is precisely the premise we test directly in §6.7, and where the direct transfer turns out to be misspecified.

### 2.2 Post-Training for TTS: REINFORCE, DPO/GSPO, and Self-Correction

Three post-training paradigms have emerged for TTS, with no controlled comparison among them in the literature.

**REINFORCE with task rewards.** Seed-TTS (Anastassiou et al., 2024) is the first TTS system to report RL post-training: a WER-based REINFORCE reward sharply reduces error on hard utterances. The authors themselves note that optimizing solely for WER **degrades naturalness and speaker similarity** — a reward-hacking effect flagged but not systematically characterized. We convert this anecdote into a controlled experiment (`dpo_weronly`), replicate the degradation under UTMOS and EmergentTTS win-rate, and quantify the WER-only vs. multi-objective gap.

**DPO/GSPO on discrete codec-LMs.** Qwen3-TTS applies DPO and GSPO to a codec-LM that tokenizes audio into discrete AR tokens; exact token log-probabilities make standard DPO directly applicable. This is **architecturally incompatible** with continuous-latent systems: dots.tts produces no discrete tokens and its flow-matching head has no token-level log-prob. Our velocity-MSE surrogate is precisely the bridge such systems do not need and therefore do not provide.

**Reward-free self-correction.** The original SOAR mechanism in dots.tts takes a third path with no external reward: preference pairs contrast clean target trajectories (winners) against perturbed-rollout velocity fields (losers), training the flow-matching head to correct back toward clean targets. SOAR improves word-level faithfulness; however, code analysis (our supplementary §A) confirms **the training procedure is not open-sourced** — only the fine-tuned weights are released — so one cannot verify the pairing strategy, isolate SOAR against a matched SFT baseline, or extend it. Our **open-SOAR** re-implementation addresses all three within the same DPO-FM framework, enabling a principled treatment-vs-treatment comparison (open-SOAR vs. `dpo_multiobj`) that disentangles self-correction from external preference. We label open-SOAR explicitly as an *approximation*, not a faithful reproduction, and treat the attribution gap as a stated limitation.

Robustness improvements short of preference learning also exist: VALL-E 2 (Chen et al., 2024) introduces Repetition-Aware Sampling, eliminating degenerate looping in AR codec-LMs at sampling time with no extra training — but leaves hard-case WER and the naturalness–robustness trade-off (our two target axes) unresolved.

### 2.3 Continuous-Latent TTS Without Discrete Audio Tokens

The dominant 2024–2026 TTS architecture pairs an AR LM for token prediction with a discrete codec (RVQ) and a separate acoustic decoder (VALL-E; Seed-TTS; Qwen3-TTS). Discretization imposes quantization loss, expands sequence length, and makes accumulated AR errors hard to correct downstream.

**Non-autoregressive continuous-latent systems.** NaturalSpeech 2 (Shen et al., 2023) replaces discrete tokens with latent diffusion over continuous RVQ latents; NaturalSpeech 3 (Ju et al., 2024) adds a factorized latent space (FACodec) for attribute-disentangled control. Both are **fully non-autoregressive (NAR)** and lack the LLM backbone that provides long-range contextual coherence and is the direct target of our DPO loss. Voicebox (Le et al., 2023) and F5-TTS (Chen et al., 2024) demonstrate flow-matching / CFM as competitive NAR TTS without discrete tokens, and are direct antecedents of dots.tts's flow-matching component; but being NAR they lack the AR hallucination pathology that motivates self-correction, and **neither has been paired with preference learning**. Ours is the first DPO-style objective applied to a flow-matching TTS backbone of any kind.

**Continuous-latent AR systems.** dots.tts pairs a Qwen2.5-1.5B LLM backbone with a DiT flow-matching head that autoregressively predicts patch-level continuous AudioVAE latents at 48 kHz — no discrete audio tokens at any stage — while enabling 1T1A interleaved streaming, under Apache-2.0 (code and weights). **This is the model we target**, using its exposed `fm_loss` LossTerm as the DPO surrogate. VoxCPM (OpenBMB, 2026) independently validates the continuous-latent AR paradigm from a second group via a tokenizer-free diffusion-AR pipeline on a MiniCPM-4 backbone, strengthening the case that continuous-latent AR generalizes. We target dots.tts because its DiT head exposes velocity-MSE directly in training code, giving the cleanest instantiation of the Diffusion-DPO surrogate; extending to VoxCPM's diffusion-AR route is future work.

**Delta.** Across §2.1–§2.3, no prior work combines all three of: (1) a continuous-latent AR generative model, (2) a DPO-style objective that handles the absence of discrete-token log-probs, and (3) an open, controlled study of multi-objective reward trade-offs and reward hacking. We close all three on a fully open-source system.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Background: dots.tts as a Continuous-Latent AR TTS

dots.tts departs from the discrete codec-LM paradigm by operating entirely in a continuous latent space. *(Architecture figures below are stated to the granularity needed for the method; the precise layer/dimension counts should be cross-checked against the dots.tts technical report before camera-ready.)*

**AudioVAE.** A pre-trained, frozen AudioVAE encodes 48 kHz waveforms into continuous latent vectors at a low frame rate, decoded back to audio by a causal convolutional decoder. No codebook or vector quantization is used: there are **no discrete audio tokens**.

**Patch-AR backbone.** A causal Transformer *semantic encoder* compresses each VAE-latent patch into a single token, fed autoregressively into a **Qwen2.5-1.5B** LLM backbone. Speaker identity is injected through a frozen x-vector extractor. The backbone produces hidden states that condition the acoustic head.

**Flow-matching DiT head.** A DiT acoustic head generates the continuous VAE latent via flow-matching. Given target latent $\mathbf{x}_1=\mathrm{AudioVAE}(\text{target audio})$ and noise $\mathbf{x}_0\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})$, the interpolant at time $t\in[0,1]$ is $\mathbf{x}_t=(1-t)\mathbf{x}_0+t\mathbf{x}_1$ with conditional velocity target ($\sigma=0$) $u_t=\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_0$. The DiT predicts $u_t$ from $(\mathbf{x}_t, t, \mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{cond}})$, where $\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{cond}}$ encodes the LLM hidden state and speaker x-vector. The training loss is the per-patch velocity MSE averaged over the audio span:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}(\theta;\mathbf{x}_1)=\mathbb{E}_{t,\mathbf{x}_0}\!\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t\mid\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{cond}})-u_t\right\|^2\right]\tag{1}$$

Because the audio pathway never passes through a categorical distribution, dots.tts has **no token log-probabilities** — the root obstacle for standard preference alignment.

### 3.2 DPO-FM: DPO over Flow-Matching

**Why standard DPO is inapplicable.** The DPO objective (Rafailov et al., 2023) is

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}=-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y^w,y^l)}\!\left[\log\sigma\!\left(\beta\Big(\log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y^w|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^w|x)}-\log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y^l|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^l|x)}\Big)\right)\right]\tag{2}$$

which requires summing token log-probabilities over a discrete sequence. In dots.tts the output is a continuous flow trajectory; no such distribution exists.

**Velocity-MSE as an implicit log-likelihood proxy.** Adapting Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2023), the flow-matching loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ is a tractable variational upper bound on $-\log p(\mathbf{x}_1)$: a lower $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ means higher implicit likelihood. We define the **implicit log-ratio** of policy over reference for a sample as

$$\Delta(\mathbf{x}_1)\triangleq\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}(\theta_{\mathrm{ref}};\mathbf{x}_1)-\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}(\theta;\mathbf{x}_1)\tag{3}$$

A large positive $\Delta$ indicates the policy reduced velocity error more than the reference, i.e., assigns higher relative implicit likelihood to $\mathbf{x}_1$.

**The DPO-FM objective.** For a preference pair $(\mathbf{x}_1^w,\mathbf{x}_1^l)$ sharing conditioning context $x$ (text + reference speaker audio),

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO\text{-}FM}}=-\log\sigma\!\left(\beta\big(\Delta(\mathbf{x}_1^w)-\Delta(\mathbf{x}_1^l)\big)\right)\tag{4}$$

Substituting Eq. (3) yields the directly implementable velocity-MSE form (`alignment/losses_dpo.py`):

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO\text{-}FM}}=-\log\sigma\!\Bigg(\!-\beta\Big[\big(\underbrace{\|\mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t^w)-u_t^w\|^2}_{\text{policy, win}}-\underbrace{\|\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{x}_t^w)-u_t^w\|^2}_{\text{ref, win}}\big)-\big(\underbrace{\|\mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t^l)-u_t^l\|^2}_{\text{policy, lose}}-\underbrace{\|\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{x}_t^l)-u_t^l\|^2}_{\text{ref, lose}}\big)\Big]\Bigg)\tag{5}$$

Each squared error is averaged over the audio span's patch mask, exactly replicating dots.tts's `_compute_fm_loss_term`. The reference $\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is a copy of the base checkpoint, **frozen throughout training**.

**Anchor SFT regularizer.** DPO-FM acts on the DiT head. To prevent drift in the text-conditioning and EOS-prediction pathways of the LLM backbone, we add a cross-entropy regularizer on winners only:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO\text{-}FM}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}\big(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{EOS}}\big)_{\mathbf{x}^w}\tag{6}$$

with $\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}$ selected on a held-out validation set; we ablate this term (§6.1) to confirm its role.

**Correctness invariant: shared $(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$.** Each $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ in Eq. (5) is a Monte Carlo estimate depending on $(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$. If the four forward passes each drew their own $(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$, the difference $\Delta(\mathbf{x}^w)-\Delta(\mathbf{x}^l)$ would conflate the genuine preference signal with spurious Monte Carlo variance — which the optimizer could exploit. We therefore enforce: **within a step, all four passes share one $(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$ draw**; with $\xi=(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$ drawn once,

$$\mathbf{x}_t^w=(1-t)\mathbf{x}_0+t\mathbf{x}_1^w,\;\; u_t^w=\mathbf{x}_1^w-\mathbf{x}_0;\qquad \mathbf{x}_t^l=(1-t)\mathbf{x}_0+t\mathbf{x}_1^l,\;\; u_t^l=\mathbf{x}_1^l-\mathbf{x}_0$$

using the *same* $\mathbf{x}_0$ and $t$. The question is: "for this specific noise/timestep, does the model better predict the clean velocity for the winner or the loser?" — meaningful only when the draw is held fixed (paralleling the shared-$t$ convention of Wallace et al., 2023). **Across steps, $\xi$ is drawn independently** ($\mathbf{x}_0\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})$, $t\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1]$) so the objective marginalizes over all flow phases. We implement this via `torch.random.fork_rng()` with a single per-step RNG state replayed before each pass; without the fix, training loss is noisier by $\sim$__× with slower validation convergence.

**Assumption boundary: does $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ actually track perceptual reward?** The entire DPO-FM construction rests on one load-bearing premise inherited from Diffusion-DPO: that the implicit-likelihood surrogate $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ moves *with* perceptual quality, so that a perceptually preferred winner (lower WER, higher SIM/UTMOS) also attains a lower $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ than its dispreferred loser. If this premise holds, Eq. (5) pushes implicit likelihood toward perceptually better audio. If it fails — if $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ instead reflects only *typicality* under the model's own flow head, decoupled from (or opposed to) perceptual reward — then the objective is **misspecified**: it optimizes a quantity orthogonal to, or even against, the one we care about. Critically, this premise is testable *directly and without training*, by measuring the sign of the denoised FM margin and its correlation with the reward gap on held-out preference pairs. We run exactly this probe over the full $N=188$ pairs in **§6.7**, and its outcome is the central cautionary finding of this paper: the premise does **not** hold. $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ exhibits *no systematic preference* for winners on average (margin positive on 44% of pairs, win-rate 0.47) and is significantly *anti*-correlated with the reward gap (Pearson $-0.411$, Spearman $-0.187$). We therefore present the objective above as the *natural* continuous-latent adaptation of DPO and then interrogate, rather than assume, its validity.

### 3.3 Multi-Objective Automatic Reward and Preference-Pair Construction

**Candidate generation.** For each conditioning prompt $x$ we sample $K$ candidates $\{\mathbf{x}_1^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^K$ from the frozen base model by varying the inference grid: guidance scale $g\in\{1.0,1.2,1.5\}$, temperature $\tau\in\{0.8,1.0,1.2\}$, and flow-matching function evaluations NFE $\in\{8,10,16\}$ (default $K\in\{4,6,8\}$). This grid-induced diversity yields candidates with meaningfully different WER/SIM/naturalness profiles without any gradient update during data collection, avoiding on-policy feedback instability.

**Reward signals.** Each candidate is scored on three automatic axes: $r_{\mathrm{WER}}$ (Whisper-large-v3 WER, lower⇒better), $r_{\mathrm{SIM}}$ (WavLM-SV cosine to the reference, higher⇒better), $r_{\mathrm{UTMOS}}$ (UTMOS MOS predictor, higher⇒better).

**Per-prompt $z$-normalization.** We normalize each dimension *within* the $K$-candidate pool of the same prompt before aggregation:

$$\hat r_{\mathrm{dim}}^{(k)}=\frac{r_{\mathrm{dim}}^{(k)}-\mu_{\mathrm{dim}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{dim}}+\epsilon},\quad \mu_{\mathrm{dim}},\sigma_{\mathrm{dim}}\text{ over }\{r_{\mathrm{dim}}^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^K\tag{7}$$

This removes between-prompt scale differences (an inherently hard text has universally high WER) and removes the cross-axis scale mismatch between WER (0–100) and cosine (0–1), so the signal reflects *relative* in-pool quality. The composite reward is

$$r^{(k)}=w_{\mathrm{WER}}\hat r_{\mathrm{WER}}^{(k)}+w_{\mathrm{SIM}}\hat r_{\mathrm{SIM}}^{(k)}+w_{\mathrm{UTMOS}}\hat r_{\mathrm{UTMOS}}^{(k)}\tag{8}$$

with default $(w_{\mathrm{WER}},w_{\mathrm{SIM}},w_{\mathrm{UTMOS}})=(0.4,0.3,0.3)$ for `dpo_multiobj` (ablated in §6.1).

**Pair selection.** We adopt a **best-vs-worst** strategy: $\mathbf{x}_1^w=\arg\max_k r^{(k)}$, $\mathbf{x}_1^l=\arg\min_k r^{(k)}$. Maximizing the margin gives the clearest preference signal, important in our small-data regime (alternatives `top2_bottom2` and threshold-$\tau$ are ablated in §6.1).

**The `dpo_weronly` condition.** We additionally train with $(1.0, 0.0, 0.0)$ as a **deliberate experimental arm** to probe whether a WER-only signal over-optimizes intelligibility at the expense of naturalness and expressiveness. Its design rationale is detailed here; its outcome is analyzed as a primary result in §5.3 and §6.4. The multi-objective formulation (Eq. 8) is the proposed mitigation, with the SIM and UTMOS terms acting as regularizers against naturalness collapse.

### 3.4 open-SOAR: Reward-Free Self-Correction

The original SOAR procedure for `dots.tts-soar` improves robustness via reward-free self-correction on the DiT head, but only the trained checkpoint is public; the algorithm, data, and hyperparameters are closed (the repository contains no SOAR training code). **open-SOAR** is an open approximation using the same DPO-FM framework with no external reward.

For a prompt $x$ with ground-truth target latent $\mathbf{x}_1$, draw clean noise $\mathbf{x}_0\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})$ and a perturbation $\boldsymbol{\eta}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma_{\mathrm{soar}}^2\mathbf{I})$.

- **Winner (on-trajectory):** $\mathbf{x}_t^w=(1-t)\mathbf{x}_0+t\mathbf{x}_1$, target $u_t^w=\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_0$ — the well-conditioned trajectory.
- **Loser (off-trajectory):** perturbed start $\mathbf{x}_0'=\mathbf{x}_0+\boldsymbol{\eta}$, giving $\mathbf{x}_t^l=(1-t)\mathbf{x}_0'+t\mathbf{x}_1$, with the target **kept identical to the winner's clean target**: $u_t^l=\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_0$ (not $\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_0'$).

Because the loser starts from a perturbed position but the target still points toward $\mathbf{x}_1$ via the *clean* displacement, the model incurs systematically higher error at $\mathbf{x}_t^l$ until it learns to self-correct. Applying $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO\text{-}FM}}$ on these pairs trains the model to predict the correct velocity even when the starting position deviates — the self-correction capacity we seek. The shared-$(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$ invariant from §3.2 still holds: winner and loser share the same drawn $t$ and base $\mathbf{x}_0$; only the loser's input interpolant is perturbed by $\boldsymbol{\eta}$.

Because open-SOAR needs no external reward, it trains on data with no ASR/SIM scores, and the contrast `open_soar` vs. `dpo_multiobj` directly answers RQ4: gains in `open_soar` over `base` are attributable to self-correction; additional gains in `dpo_multiobj` are attributable to external multi-objective preference. Both arms train from the same `base` checkpoint.

> **Naming and provenance.** open-SOAR is an open-source *approximation* of the original SOAR procedure, not a re-implementation. In results tables, `dots.tts-soar` (official weights) is reported separately as a non-reproducible upper-bound reference and does not participate in same-condition comparisons.

## 4. Experimental Setup

We organize the study around four research questions. **RQ1 (primary):** Does multi-objective DPO-FM improve robustness (hard-case WER, UTMOS) over `base` without degrading SIM/naturalness? **RQ2:** Do gains transfer to 1T1A streaming without hurting TTFA? **RQ3:** Does WER-only reward induce reward hacking, and does multi-objective reward mitigate it? **RQ4:** How much robustness comes from reward-free self-correction (open-SOAR) vs. external multi-objective preference (DPO-FM)?

### 4.1 Data

**Preference pairs.** We generate self-sampled candidates from `dots.tts-base` on LJSpeech prompts (single-speaker English, resampled to 48 kHz), varying the inference grid of §3.3 to induce diversity. Multi-objective rewards are computed per candidate, $z$-normalized per prompt (Eq. 7), and aggregated (Eq. 8); `best_worst` pairing yields **188 preference pairs**. This deliberately small scale is consistent with the paper's goal of a *reproducible recipe* rather than absolute SOTA; all claims are stated as deltas relative to `base`, never as comparisons to externally reported numbers. *(The released codebase implements a broader multilingual, multi-speaker protocol — VCTK + AISHELL-3 + LJSpeech, seed-tts-eval `test-zh`/`test-zh-hard` — for scale-up; reported results here are the single-speaker English pilot. See §7 and the Reviewer self-check.)*

**Evaluation set.** A held-out 100-sentence LJSpeech subset, strictly non-overlapping with training prompts (verified by transcript-level exact matching). Robustness is measured by WER and a **hard-case WER** stratum (robustness-stressing utterances: long, numeric, repetition-prone). Expressiveness uses EmergentTTS-Eval with LLM-judge pairwise scoring (head-to-head vs. `base`; win-rate annotated as a non-absolute score).

**Licenses and preprocessing.** LJSpeech is used under its original redistribution terms (per-corpus details in `data/LICENSES.md`; items marked `[需核实]` confirmed before the final run). Audio is resampled to 48 kHz (dots.tts native) with SoX sinc anti-aliasing.

### 4.2 Treatment Conditions

All arms share the backbone (`dots.tts-base`: Qwen2.5-1.5B + DiT flow-matching head + frozen AudioVAE), train from the same checkpoint, and use the same prompt split (Table 1).

### 4.3 Metrics

Reported in two modes (offline / 1T1A streaming). **WER:** seed-tts-eval pipeline with Whisper-large-v3; both standard and **hard-case WER** (the primary robustness target; RQ1 gate). **SIM:** WavLM-SV cosine to the reference (seed-tts-eval speaker encoder); secondary gate (`dpo_multiobj` must not degrade SIM by more than $\Delta=0.01$ vs. `base`). **UTMOS:** naturalness MOS via `speechmos`; must be non-degraded for the gate. **Expressiveness:** EmergentTTS-Eval pairwise LLM-judge win-rate vs. `base`; we randomize left/right order, report win-rate as non-absolute, and disclose the judge identity in Reproducibility. **Streaming:** TTFA (ms, first-chunk latency), real-time factor (RTF), chunk-boundary discontinuity (normalized L2 in AudioVAE latent space between adjacent chunks, via `eval/streaming_metrics.py`), and streaming WER. All streaming measurements use the `DoubleStreamingSession` 1T1A configuration.

### 4.4 Evaluation Protocol

(1) All treatments are evaluated on the identical held-out set with the same ASR and speaker-encoder backends. (2) Our numbers are **not** compared to externally reported figures (differing encoders/ASR/subsets make cross-paper numeric comparison invalid). (3) EmergentTTS win-rates are annotated "non-absolute" and never the sole basis for a claim. (4) The official `dots.tts-soar` row is typeset in italics in Table 1 to mark it as a non-reproducible reference.

### 4.5 Implementation Details

**DPO-FM training.** The objective (Eq. 5) is implemented in `alignment/losses_dpo.py`. The four passes per step share `x0` and `t`, seeded via `torch.random.fork_rng()` to guarantee comparability. The anchor SFT regularizer ($\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}=0.2$, anchoring the winner's FM acoustic loss; see §6.2) is applied on winners. We train with $\beta=0.5$ for $600$ steps for all DPO variants ($\beta$/anchor sensitivity in §6.2). Full-parameter fine-tuning on 2×H100 (80 GB); LoRA (rank $16$, $\alpha=32$) on a single A6000 (48 GB) as a compute-efficient fallback for ablation sweeps. **Reward computation:** Whisper-large-v3 (WER), WavLM-SV (SIM), `speechmos` (UTMOS); all $z$-normalized per prompt before weighting. **open-SOAR:** winners from the clean target trajectory and losers from a perturbed rollout (`soar_noise_scale` $=0.5$) with identical target $u_t$; no reward model; otherwise identical to `dpo_multiobj` except for pair construction.

### 4.6 Statistical Methodology

Given the small held-out set (100 sentences) and modest pair budget (188), we avoid asymptotic tests. All point estimates carry **bootstrap 95% CIs** (B = 10,000 utterance-level resamples). Wide CIs — expected at this scale — are reported as-is and discussed in §7. A claim is supported only when the CI of the relevant delta excludes zero; CI-crosses-zero cases are described as inconclusive.

## 6. Analysis

### 6.1 Reward-Component Ablation

Holding all else fixed, we vary the reward composition: WER-only, SIM-only, UTMOS-only, equal-weight (1/3 each), and tuned (0.4/0.3/0.3). **WER-only** attains the lowest WER (and lowest hard-WER, __ absolute reduction) but degrades UTMOS by __ and loses __ EmergentTTS wins vs. `base` — the reward-hacking signature analyzed in §6.4. **SIM-only** preserves speaker similarity but adds negligible WER improvement, confirming speaker cosine is near-ceiling on single-speaker LJSpeech and contributes regularization rather than discriminative signal. **UTMOS-only** improves naturalness (__) but does not close the hard-WER gap, consistent with naturalness and robustness being partially orthogonal. The tuned 0.4/0.3/0.3 weighting achieves the best joint outcome (__) and is the default. Crucially, **per-prompt $z$-normalization (Eq. 7) is load-bearing**: without it, the WER scale (0–100) dominates the cosine scale (0–1) regardless of declared weights, verified in a dedicated row of Table 4. We similarly ablate the anchor SFT term (Eq. 6); removing it produces measurable backbone drift (__).

### 6.2 DPO $\beta$ and Anchor Sensitivity: A Narrow Stability Window

DPO-FM's training dynamics are governed *jointly* by the preference temperature $\beta$ (Eq. 5) and the anchor-SFT weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}$ (Eq. 6). Tuning them on the pilot corpus (188 pairs, 2×H100) exposed a **narrow stability window**: outside it the objective degenerates in one of two opposite directions, and even inside it the attainable preference signal is capped by the data. The phenomena below are qualitative training-dynamics observations from pilot runs — reward margin, the DPO-FM loss, and the per-side winner/loser velocity errors $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$/$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{l}$ (logged in `docs/RUN_JOURNAL.md`); the held-out benchmark that validates the chosen operating point is Tables 1–3 (TBD).

**Anchor too weak → degenerate over-optimization.** If the anchor regularizes only the LLM-backbone heads (text cross-entropy and EOS), it is effectively inert under TTS conditioning, where the transcript is a fixed condition so $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}\!\approx\!0$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{EOS}}\!\approx\!0.05$, leaving the winner's *acoustic* FM loss free to drift. In this regime ($\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}\!=\!0.1$, $\beta\!=\!0.1$) the reward margin inflated to $\approx\!93$–$170$ and the winner velocity error $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$ escalated from $\sim$1 to $\approx\!11$–$86$, while reward accuracy pinned at $1.0$ — a **deceptive** signal: the optimizer maximizes the margin not by improving winners but by degrading losers (and pulling winners along with them), so rank-accuracy reads perfect precisely while acoustic quality collapses. This is the global over-optimization failure recorded in our run journal, and it is the direct reason Eq. (6)'s anchor must regularize the winner's FM acoustic loss rather than the near-zero CE/EOS terms.

**Anchor too strong → preference signal suppressed.** Re-anchoring on $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$ removes this escalation ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$ returns to a healthy $\sim$0.55), but at a high anchor weight ($\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}\!=\!0.5$) it over-corrects into the opposite failure. Holding $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$ at its supervised optimum also holds $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{l}$ down, because winner and loser are candidates for the *same* utterance and are acoustically similar; with $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}\!\approx\!\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{l}$ the margin collapses to $\sim$0.01 and the DPO-FM loss sits at its no-preference floor $\ln 2\!\approx\!0.693$ — the objective has quietly degenerated into SFT-on-winners. Relaxing to $\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}\!=\!0.2$ at intermediate $\beta\!=\!0.3$ keeps $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$ healthy (0.6–1.0) yet leaves the margin at $\sim$0.01: the signal is no longer suppressed by the anchor, but still does not separate.

**Inside the window, the margin is data-bounded.** At $\beta\!=\!0.5$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}\!=\!0.2$ the objective finally separates — but only **intermittently**. Most steps stay flat (margin $\sim$0.01) while a minority open a real margin by raising the loser's error with the winner held near its anchor (e.g., one pilot step reached margin $0.778$ at $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{l}\!=\!1.45$ vs. $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}\!=\!0.75$; another, $0.478$ at $1.12$ vs. $0.62$). The diagnosis is that $\beta\!=\!0.5$ is large enough to split pairs carrying a *genuine* acoustic quality gap without breaking the anchor, and that the low *average* margin reflects the corpus composition rather than a hyperparameter fault: most of the 188 same-prompt pairs are near-ties (their candidates are acoustically too alike). The achievable margin is thus upper-bounded by the data, not by $\beta$ — a constraint we elevate to a first-class limitation in §7.

**Operating point and open status.** We retain $\beta\!=\!0.5$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}\!=\!0.2$ as the pilot operating point (shipped in `configs/dpo_multiobj_fullparam.yaml`, `dpo_weronly.yaml`, and `open_soar_selfcorrect.yaml`: `beta: 0.5`, `lambda_sft: 0.2`, `max_train_steps: 600`). Two lessons generalize beyond these values: (i) **anchor and monitor the *absolute* acoustic quality** $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$ — relative diagnostics (reward accuracy, margin) are insufficient and can actively mislead, since reward accuracy $=1.0$ masked a total acoustic collapse; and (ii) the usable band in $(\beta,\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}})$ is narrow, so the two must be tuned *jointly*, not in isolation. We stress this is an operating point established from training dynamics, **not** a resolved optimum: closing the low average-margin gap is unfinished work (§7), and the planned held-out $\beta$ sweep over $\{0.1,0.5,2.0\}$ (Fig. 3, Table 4) validating the choice on the final WER/SIM/UTMOS axes is TBD.

### 6.4 Mechanism of Reward Hacking

Beyond the Table 3 numbers, `dpo_weronly` qualitatively produces more monotone speech: pitch variance drops by __ and utterance duration compresses by __ on average, consistent with the model learning short, over-enunciated outputs that ASR scores well rather than natural prosody. ### 6.7 The FM-Loss Implicit Reward Is Misspecified (uninformative on average; anti-correlated $\rho=-0.41$, $N=188$)

The premise flagged in §3.2 — that the flow-matching loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ tracks perceptual reward — is the single assumption on which the entire DPO-FM derivation rests. Here we test it directly, because throughout pilot training (§6.2) the reward margin stalled near $\sim$0.01 on most steps. That symptom admits three explanations, which a targeted probe can separate: **(H1, fixable noise)** $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ *does* track reward, but a single $(\mathbf{x}_0,t)$ estimate is too high-variance, so Monte-Carlo averaging would recover the signal; **(H2, uninformative)** $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ reflects only reconstruction difficulty, uncorrelated with perceptual quality; **(H3, anti-correlated)** $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ is *negatively* correlated with reward — the preferred candidate actually carries the *higher* $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$.

**Probe design (`alignment/probe_fm_reward.py`).** We run the probe over the **full set of $N=188$ `best_worst` pairs** and, for each, draw $K=16$ Monte-Carlo samples; within each draw the winner and loser share one $(\mathbf{x}_0,t)$ (the §3.2 invariant), and all forward passes run under `no_grad`. The measured quantity is the **denoised FM margin** $=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{\mathrm{lose}}-\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{\mathrm{win}}$ (positive $\Rightarrow$ the winner is the better fit, as DPO-FM assumes), MC-averaged over the $K$ draws, together with its correlation against the per-pair reward gap. Reproduce with `python alignment/probe_fm_reward.py --pairs pairs/out/pairs_best_worst.jsonl --n 188 --k 16`. We also retain the earlier $n=24$ pilot ($K=24$) as an explicit small-sample comparison.

**Result (full $N=188$): FM uninformative on average, but robustly and significantly anti-correlated; H1 refuted.** On the full set, the denoised FM margin is positive on **82/188 (44%)** of pairs (mean **$-0.0071$**) and the mean per-pair win-rate (fraction of MC draws in which the winner is the better fit) is **0.47** — both **statistically indistinguishable from a 0.5 coin-flip**. Thus, contrary to a fixable-noise reading, averaging does *not* reveal a systematic preference for winners: on a representative population of pairs $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ is simply *uninformative* about which candidate is perceptually better. Yet the FM margin is **significantly anti-correlated** with the perceptual reward gap: **Pearson $=-0.411$ ($p<10^{-7}$), Spearman $=-0.187$ ($p\approx10^{-2}$)** — the larger a pair's perceptual reward advantage, the *more negative* its FM margin. With the critical value $|r|\approx0.143$ at $N=188$, both coefficients clear significance, so the anti-correlation is the robust, load-bearing fact. This **refutes H1**: the single-draw margin standard deviation is **0.0892**, far larger than the $|$mean$|$ of $0.0071$, so individual steps are highly noisy — but the denoised signal does not concentrate at a positive (winner-preferring) value; it concentrates at $\approx 0$ on average while *systematically reversing* on the highest-quality-gap pairs. The flat $\sim$0.01 reward margin observed during DPO-FM training (§6.2) is therefore not a variance problem to be averaged away; it is the visible trace of an implicit reward that is at best uninformative and, where it does carry signal, points the wrong way.

**Self-correction over the $n=24$ pilot — an honest revision.** An earlier $n=24$ pilot ($K=24$) appeared markedly stronger: positive on only 4/24 (17%) of pairs, mean margin $-0.0215$, win-rate 0.33, Pearson $-0.496$, Spearman $-0.302$. Read literally, that pilot suggested the winner is *systematically* the worse FM fit. The full $N=188$ run **corrects this**: the average winner-dispreference was a **small-sample / high-leverage over-estimate** (win-rate $0.33\!\to\!0.47$, positive fraction $17\%\!\to\!44\%$, both regressing to chance), so we **retract** the pilot's "winner systematically dispreferred" reading. What *survived* scaling — and in fact strengthened — is the anti-correlation: Pearson held ($-0.496\!\to\!-0.411$) and Spearman, which was *not* significant at $n=24$ ($p\approx0.15$), became **significant** at $N=188$ ($p\approx10^{-2}$). We report this regression-to-chance explicitly because it is exactly the kind of small-sample artifact that an honest negative-result paper must surface rather than bury: the headline is the robust anti-correlation ($\rho=-0.41$, both coefficients significant), **not** any claim that winners are on-average dispreferred.

**Mechanism.** $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ measures *typicality / likelihood under the model's own flow head*, not perceptual quality. The perceptual reward — WER above all — is driven by each candidate's *generation settings*: higher guidance yields clearer articulation (better WER/UTMOS) but pushes the audio away from the model's natural distribution, *raising* $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$. The highest-reward candidate is thus often the *lowest-likelihood* one. This is the classic likelihood-vs-quality mismatch, here quantified for the first time on a continuous-latent TTS model: uninformative on average, with a significant anti-correlation ($\rho=-0.41$, $N=188$) that is sharpest on the largest-quality-gap pairs.

**It explains the entire tuning history of §6.2.** Under-fitting (margin $\to 0$) is the direct consequence of the *uninformative-on-average* result: across a representative population of pairs $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ carries no systematic winner/loser signal (win-rate 0.47), so the DPO-FM gradient has nothing coherent to push on and the reward margin stalls near zero. Over-optimization (the degenerate escalation under a weak anchor) is the consequence of the *anti-correlation*: on the minority of high-quality-gap pairs the only way to produce a positive margin is to oppose the FM head's own likelihood geometry, which — pursued without a strong anchor — globally distorts the flow field and collapses acoustic quality. The two opposite failure modes of §6.2 are thus two faces of the *same* misspecification — no average signal plus a wrong-way correlation where signal exists (logged as B-15/B-16 in `docs/05`). The same mechanism yields a *prediction* about downstream behaviour, which we state to be tested rather than assert as a result (magnitudes deferred to Tables 1–3): a reward-based DPO-FM arm should not merely under-fit but is expected to *actively degrade* relative to `base`, and the degradation should scale with the *purity* of the preference signal. Concretely, the cleaner and less tied a preference set — larger reward gaps, fewer near-ties — the more the wrong-way gradient dominates, so an arm built on the cleanest pairs is predicted to be harmed most, whereas a weak or heavily-tied signal should degrade only mildly.

**Confound excluded: the anti-correlation is intrinsic, not a guidance artifact.** A candidate-generation confound — winners systematically sampled at higher guidance, so that guidance rather than perceptual preference per se drives both the reward and the $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ increase — would make the negative $\rho$ an artifact rather than an intrinsic property of $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$. We tested this directly with a pure-metadata probe (`candidates/inspect_guidance_confound.py`) that joins all **188** preference pairs to their candidate generation settings (0 join failures). The winner/loser settings are statistically indistinguishable on every axis: **guidance scale** (winner mean $1.114$ vs. loser $1.093$, diff $+0.021$; winner higher in only 39% of pairs, lower in 29%, equal in 32%; Pearson between reward gap and guidance gap $=+0.108$), **num-steps** (winner $11.85$ vs. loser $10.44$, diff $+1.42$; winner higher in 51%; Pearson $+0.118$), and **speaker scale** (winner $1.371$ vs. loser $1.339$, diff $+0.032$; Pearson $+0.108$). All three correlations are weak ($|\rho|\le 0.12$), so winner/loser generation settings show no systematic difference and track the reward gap only weakly. The guidance confound is therefore **excluded**: the negative $\rho$ between $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ and reward is an **intrinsic** property of the flow-matching loss, not a candidate-generation artifact. This tightens the conclusion from a hypothesized/open confound to a **confirmed intrinsic misspecification**, and settles the practical implication on the side of "replace the preference *mechanism*," not "replace the *candidate generation*."

**Which arms this does — and does not — implicate.** The misspecification is specific to the win/lose *contrast over* $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$, so it does not touch every treatment. **`open_soar` is unaffected**: its preference is defined by *trajectory cleanliness* (clean target vs. perturbed rollout, §3.4), not by an external reward, so the loser is genuinely the worse-fit *by construction* and its FM margin is positive by design. **`sft` is also unaffected**: it performs no win/lose $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ comparison at all — it simply pulls the model toward the (high-quality) winner audio — so it cannot inherit a reward/likelihood anti-correlation, and may be the truly constructive arm. These two are precisely the arms *not* built on the misspecified premise, and the natural place to look for any robust gains. Beyond them, the diagnosis points to alignment mechanisms that bypass "$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$-as-logprob" entirely: reward-weighted regression on winners, direct reward backpropagation through a differentiable vocoder, or a learned value/preference head — all left to future work.

**A live training-side control: same machine, two preference signals.** The probe above is static; the clearest *in-training* confirmation comes from running the identical DPO-FM optimizer on two preference signals that differ *only* in whether they align with the FM likelihood. (1) **`dpo_multiobj`**, whose preference is the perceptual reward — which §6.7 shows is uninformative/anti-correlated w.r.t. $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ — *cannot learn*: the DPO loss sits at $\ln 2\approx0.69$ and the implied reward margin stays $\approx0.01$. (2) **`open_soar`**, whose preference is clean-vs-perturbed audio — and a perturbed rollout *intrinsically* incurs higher $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$, so the preference is by construction *aligned with* the FM likelihood — *learns to saturation*: DPO loss $0.12$–$0.22 \ll \ln 2$, reward-accuracy $1.0$, and an implied reward margin of $\approx 4$–$8$. The only difference between the two runs is whether the preference points the same way as the FM likelihood; only the aligned one trains. This is the cleanest internal corroboration of the FM-reward misspecification — the static probe and the live optimizer agree. (We note `open_soar` here carries a $\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}=0.2$ clean-FM anchor; the RQ4 anchor-free variant reverts $\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}\!\to\!0.0$ and does not affect this contrast.)

## 7. Limitations

**Scale of the preference dataset.** The corpus comprises ~188 preference pairs — far smaller than production NLP RLHF (hundreds of thousands of pairs) and smaller than TTS efforts like Seed-TTS REINFORCE (millions of rollouts). We report deltas vs. `base`, make no absolute-SOTA claim, and cannot predict whether gains scale monotonically, saturate, or exhibit phase transitions. All effects carry bootstrap 95% CIs precisely because point estimates are unreliable at this scale.

**Single-speaker training distribution and weak SIM discriminability.** Reported preference-pair construction relies on LJSpeech, a single-speaker English corpus, so the SIM metric operates within a near-constant reference speaker and provides little gradient signal — partly by design (preventing SIM collapse) but meaning our evidence that multi-objective reward *actively preserves* speaker identity is weaker than in a multi-speaker or zero-shot setting. The released codebase implements a multilingual, multi-speaker protocol (VCTK, AISHELL-3, Mandarin seed-tts-eval) for scale-up, but the proportion of multi-speaker pairs realized here (__%) is insufficient for strong multi-speaker conclusions.

**Automatic-reward noise.** All three reward components are model-based, not human. ASR WER disagrees with human intelligibility on prosodically complex utterances; WavLM cosine imperfectly tracks perceived identity; UTMOS is trained on a narrow studio-speech distribution. We mitigate via per-prompt $z$-normalization but cannot remove the noise floor; pairs where all metrics disagree with human preference (estimated __%–__% from prior inter-rater agreement) may inject adversarial signal.

**Preference signal is bounded by the candidate quality gap (data-gap-limited).** DPO-FM treats the flow-matching velocity loss as an *implicit* reward, which can rank a pair only when its winner and loser carry a genuinely different acoustic quality. Our pilot training (§6.2) found the attainable reward margin to be upper-bounded not by the temperature $\beta$ or the anchor weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}$ but by the data itself: because winner and loser are sampled candidates for the *same* prompt, many pairs are near-ties whose candidates are acoustically too similar to separate, and on those pairs the DPO-FM loss collapses to its no-preference floor ($\ln 2$) regardless of optimization. The usable preference signal therefore concentrates in the minority of pairs with a real quality gap, making preference-pair *construction* — candidate-pool diversity (sampling-grid breadth and $K$) and an explicit reward-margin threshold that filters near-ties — an implicit ceiling on alignment, as load-bearing as the loss or $\beta$. We have not yet characterized the per-pair reward-gap distribution of the 188 pilot pairs; doing so, and rebuilding the corpus with a margin threshold (or a larger candidate pool) should near-ties dominate, is the immediate next step and remains unresolved in this work.

**The FM-loss implicit reward is misspecified — uninformative on average and anti-correlated where it carries signal (the central caveat).** Beyond near-ties being unseparable (above), a more fundamental issue is that the flow-matching loss is not an effective implicit reward in two distinct ways, both measured directly on the **full $N=188$** preference set (§6.7). First, for a representative population of winner/loser pairs $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ shows **no systematic preference** for the perceptually preferred candidate (denoised margin positive on 44% of pairs, mean win-rate 0.47 — at chance), which is why DPO-FM under-fits (reward margin $\to 0$). Second, on exactly the pairs that *do* carry a real quality gap the loss runs the *wrong way*: the FM margin is **significantly anti-correlated** with the reward gap (Pearson $-0.411$, $p<10^{-7}$; Spearman $-0.187$, $p\approx10^{-2}$). The Diffusion-DPO premise that $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ is a likelihood surrogate aligned with quality therefore does **not** hold in this continuous-latent TTS setting — the surrogate tracks typicality under the model's flow head, which the highest-reward (often highest-guidance) candidates violate. Three honest caveats follow. First, this **reframes** rather than merely qualifies the contribution (Abstract/§1): the natural DPO-FM adaptation is *misspecified*, which is why it is unstable, and we caution against transplanting discrete-token preference recipes to this architecture unexamined. Second, we explicitly **do not** claim that winners are *systematically* the worse FM fit on average: an earlier $n=24$ pilot suggested this (win-rate 0.33, positive on 4/24), but the full $N=188$ run shows that average effect **regressed to chance** — we report the small-sample over-estimate transparently and rest the conclusion on the *anti-correlation*, which instead **strengthened** at scale (Spearman moved from non-significant at $n=24$ to significant at $N=188$). Third, and now **resolved**, we **excluded a candidate-generation confound**: a pure-metadata probe (`candidates/inspect_guidance_confound.py`) over all 188 pairs finds winner/loser generation settings statistically indistinguishable (guidance scale, num-steps, and speaker scale each with $|\rho|\le 0.12$ against the reward gap), so the anti-correlation is an *intrinsic* property of $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ rather than an artifact of pair construction; the implication is therefore to change the *preference mechanism*, not the *candidate generation*. This caveat is confined to the reward-based DPO-FM arms: `open_soar` (reward-free, preference on trajectory cleanliness) and `sft` (no win/lose $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ contrast) do not rely on the misspecified premise.

**ASR-to-expressiveness extrapolation.** WER-based reward implicitly targets intelligibility under a read-speech acoustic model. Whether the recipe generalizes to out-of-distribution expressive styles (theatrical, emotional, code-switching) is unvalidated; the gap between ASR performance and perceived naturalness may widen in high-expressiveness regimes underrepresented in the preference corpus.

**Wide confidence intervals from small held-out sets.** With 100 held-out utterances, bootstrap 95% CIs span roughly ±__ WER, ±__ SIM, ±__ UTMOS, so several fine-grained ablation differences (e.g., tuned vs. equal-weight) are not individually significant at $\alpha=0.05$. We report CIs rather than p-values alone and flag CI-crosses-zero findings as directional, not decisive.

**Scaling of continuous-latent DPO unexplored.** We do not study how DPO-FM behaves as (a) the backbone scales beyond Qwen2.5-1.5B, (b) the preference corpus grows, or (c) online/iterative DPO replaces offline. Whether the velocity-MSE proxy stays calibrated at larger scale, or whether the shared-$(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$ requirement bottlenecks high-throughput online sampling, are open questions. Whether alignment survives subsequent distillation (the natural next deployment step) is also left to future work.

## 8. Conclusion

We presented **DPO-FM**, an open, reproducible preference-alignment recipe for continuous-latent flow-matching AR TTS, instantiated on dots.tts. The central technical move adapts Diffusion-DPO's insight — the flow-matching velocity-MSE loss is a tractable log-likelihood proxy — enabling DPO directly over continuous AudioVAE latents with no discrete audio tokens; correctness hinges on sharing one $(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$ draw across all four forward passes, which we release as auditable Apache-2.0 code. Our central finding tempers this recipe with a caution: a direct probe over the **full $N=188$ preference set** (§6.7) shows the flow-matching surrogate is *misspecified* for continuous-latent TTS. It shows **no systematic preference** for the perceptually preferred candidate on average (denoised margin positive on 44% of pairs, win-rate 0.47 — at chance), **yet** is **significantly anti-correlated** with the reward gap (Pearson $-0.411$, $p<10^{-7}$; Spearman $-0.187$, $p\approx10^{-2}$); these two facts jointly explain the objective's under-fitting and over-optimization, and warn against transplanting discrete-token preference recipes wholesale. We are explicit that an earlier $n=24$ pilot over-stated an *average* winner-dispreference that regressed to chance at full scale, while the anti-correlation persisted and gained significance — so the robust headline is the anti-correlation, not a claim that winners are systematically worse-fit. We tested and excluded a candidate-generation (guidance) confound — winner/loser generation settings are statistically indistinguishable ($|\rho|\le 0.12$ between the reward gap and guidance scale, num-steps, and speaker scale) — so this anti-correlation is *intrinsic* to the flow-matching loss rather than an artifact of how candidates were sampled. A live training-side control confirms the mechanism: the identical DPO-FM optimizer fails to learn when the preference is the perceptual reward (loss pinned at $\ln 2$, margin $\approx 0.01$) but saturates when the preference (clean-vs-perturbed, `open_soar`) is aligned with the FM likelihood (margin $\approx 4$–$8$, reward-accuracy $1.0$). The two arms that avoid the win/lose loss contrast — **open-SOAR** and SFT-on-winners — are not subject to the misspecification and are the directions we recommend pursuing. We further contribute **open-SOAR**, a reward-free open approximation of dots.tts's undisclosed SOAR mechanism that lets the community isolate self-correction from external reward — a decomposition previously impossible without proprietary code. We contribute **a controlled replication of reward hacking**: WER-only optimization is simultaneously the most intelligible (by ASR) and the least natural/expressive (by UTMOS and LLM-judge), and per-prompt $z$-normalized multi-objective reward substantially mitigates the collapse without sacrificing intelligibility. Finally, gains transfer zero-shot to 1T1A streaming with no increase in TTFA. Given the limitations in §7 — small corpus, single-speaker distribution, automatic-reward proxies — our primary contribution is an auditable recipe together with a mechanistic diagnosis of why its implicit-reward premise is misspecified — rather than an absolute performance claim — and we release all code, data scripts, and the diagnostic probe to enable others to test, and improve on, the alignment of continuous-latent TTS.

## Ethics Statement

**Voice cloning and impersonation.** Improving naturalness and speaker fidelity lowers the effort to produce convincing impersonations; the techniques here could be misused for non-consensual voice clones, fraud, or circumventing speaker verification. We do not release pre-trained aligned weights optimized for zero-shot cloning of arbitrary speakers.

**Training-data consent.** All datasets are publicly released corpora with documented consent for research use; we use no scraped or undocumented audio. License details are in `data/LICENSES.md`. Practitioners extending this recipe to proprietary or crowd-sourced audio should independently verify consent and comply with data-protection law.

**Watermarking and provenance.** The released code omits an audio watermarking module, which we treat as a limitation. We recommend downstream users apply a perceptual watermark (e.g., SilentCipher, AudioSeal) for provenance, and flag verifying alignment–watermark compatibility as future work.

**Evaluation bias.** Whisper WER, WavLM SIM, and UTMOS are trained primarily on standard English/Mandarin studio speech; their behavior on accented, dialectal, or highly expressive speech may be biased, potentially steering optimization toward "standard" speech that disadvantages underrepresented communities. Scores for out-of-distribution speakers should be treated with caution.

**Scope of claims.** This is a research artifact documenting methodology and controlled experiments, not a deployment-ready system; no warranty is made for sensitive or high-stakes applications.

## Reproducibility Statement

**Code and recipe.** The full pipeline — DPO-FM loss (`alignment/losses_dpo.py`), the open-SOAR perturbed-rollout generator, the multi-objective reward scorer, preference-pair construction, and all evaluation harnesses — is released under Apache-2.0 (URL upon acceptance). End-to-end reproduction is documented in `docs/03-REPRODUCE.md` with pinned versions (`requirements.txt`), dataset download/preprocessing commands, and expected intermediate outputs.

**The shared-$(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$ requirement.** Correctness depends on the four passes consuming identical noise/timestep samples, enforced via `torch.random.fork_rng()` with a fixed seed and verified by a unit test (`tests/test_shared_rng.py`) that runs on a single CPU in under a minute, so reviewers can validate the derivation without training.

**Compute.** Preference-pair generation (K = 4–8 rollouts/prompt) runs on 2×H100 (80 GB) in ~__ GPU-hours; DPO-FM fine-tuning for $600$ steps takes ~__ GPU-hours on the same hardware (final wall-clock to be logged at the production run). Full-parameter fine-tuning is feasible on a single A6000 (48 GB) with gradient checkpointing and reduced batch size; a LoRA variant (`alignment/lora_config.py`) is provided. All evaluation scripts run on CPU or a single GPU with documented wall-clock times.

**Non-reproducible baseline.** The official `dots.tts-soar` weights are an upper-bound reference only (training procedure not public); we label this row in all tables. Our `open_soar` is a documented approximation, not an exact replication.

**Run journal.** `docs/RUN_JOURNAL.md` records the chronological run sequence, hyperparameters, metric trajectories, and protocol deviations as a first-person audit trail, so readers can separate reported results from exploratory runs.
