# When Likelihood Disagrees with Quality: A Cautionary Diagnosis of Direct Preference Optimization for Continuous-Latent Flow-Matching TTS (dots.tts)

*Alternative titles considered (for author decision): "Likelihood Is Not Reward: Why the Natural DPO Adaptation Is the Wrong Tool for Off-Manifold Preferences in dots.tts"; "A Likelihood-vs-Quality Gap in Continuous-Latent Flow-Matching TTS: A Diagnosis on dots.tts, with a Likelihood-Aligned Self-Correction Alternative (open-SOAR)."*

## Abstract

Continuous-latent autoregressive (AR) TTS — exemplified by dots.tts, which generates audio through a flow-matching DiT head over a continuous AudioVAE with **no discrete audio tokens** — is robust, but its preference alignment is unsolved: standard Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) requires token log-probabilities that continuous-latent systems do not have. We study the *natural* adaptation, **DPO-FM**, which (following Diffusion-DPO) substitutes dots.tts's flow-matching velocity-MSE loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ as an implicit likelihood surrogate, using four shared-RNG forward passes to keep the policy/reference comparison valid. Our central result is cautionary and diagnostic, and we are careful to localize precisely *what* fails. The surrogate itself remains a **valid likelihood proxy** — a likelihood-aligned preference (open-SOAR, below) optimizes it to saturation. What fails is the *coupling* between an automatic-metric reward preference and that likelihood: on dots.tts, a direct, training-free probe over the **full $N=188$** best-vs-worst preference set finds the denoised FM margin **weakly but significantly monotonically anti-correlated** with the automatic-metric reward gap (**Spearman $\rho=-0.187$, $p\approx10^{-2}$; $\rho^2\approx3.5\%$, weak by Cohen's convention**; a leverage-amplified Pearson $-0.411$ is reported alongside). In words, the candidate that the quality reward prefers tends to be the *more atypical (off-manifold)* member of its pair under the model's own flow head, hence carries the *higher* $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ — a textbook likelihood-vs-quality / classifier-free-guidance off-manifold gap, here quantified for a continuous-latent flow-matching TTS model. This is a property of the *preference–likelihood coupling*, not of $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ as a likelihood estimator, and it is **intrinsic** to that coupling rather than a generation-grid artifact: a partial correlation that controls for all three sampling knobs (`alignment/probe_analysis.py`) leaves it essentially unchanged (raw Pearson $-0.411\to$ partial $-0.392$, 95% CI $[-0.548,-0.209]$, significant; only num-steps/NFE is independently confounded, $-0.237$, yet the effect survives partialling it out). The practical implication: likelihood-based DPO is the wrong tool when the reward preference is off-manifold, whereas a likelihood-aligned preference (open-SOAR) or non-likelihood objectives (reward-weighted regression, direct reward backprop through a differentiable vocoder, a learned value head) are the right path. We report an honest revision: an earlier $n=24$ pilot over-stated an *average* winner-dispreference (apparent win-rate 0.33) that regressed toward chance at full scale (82/188 positive, not significantly below 0.5, $p=0.093$), whereas the anti-correlation persisted and *gained* significance. Held-out $n{=}100$ results sharpen this into a broader, more cautionary thesis. Of five arms, **only reward-free self-correction (open-SOAR)** — whose target is the *real, on-manifold* recording — improves (best UTMOS 4.408, WER 0.093 ≈ base 0.103); every arm trained toward *self-generated, automatic-reward-selected* targets degrades, **including SFT-on-winners** (WER collapses 0.103→0.545, UTMOS 4.309→3.917), which we had expected to be safe and which a reviewer singled out as the "truly unaffected control." The damage is monotone in how hard each arm pulls toward those off-manifold winners (open-SOAR < `dpo_weronly` ≈ base < `sft` < `dpo_multiobj`, the last collapsing to WER 0.752 with a 25% immediate-EOS generation-failure rate). With explicit caveats — the SFT collapse is confounded with ordinary small-data SFT instability; the `dpo_multiobj` failure is a generation-time collapse on a prompt subset whose graded-vs-divergence character is mixed (its training curve did *not* re-diverge, yet the FM margin decouples from reward on the largest-gap pairs), reported conservatively pending manual adjudication; the failures concentrate in the `LJ002` block, which is itself 89% of the eval set — the cautionary generalization is that **self-training on automatic-reward-selected off-manifold candidates harms the model regardless of the objective (DPO or SFT)**, and only training toward genuine on-manifold targets helps. The planned controlled reward-hacking comparison (WER-only vs. multi-objective) **could not be reproduced**: the mis-specified DPO-FM objective never reaches the low-WER regime where hacking would occur (the WER-only arm does *not* attain the lowest WER), so the reward-composition effect is confounded by, and dominated by, the FM collapse — we report this as a negative result. Whether offline alignment transfers to one-token-one-audio (1T1A) streaming without added first-chunk latency remains the one forthcoming axis (Table 2, streaming, still [TBD]; this run used `--skip-streaming`). To our knowledge this is **among the first** preference-alignment studies on any continuous-latent flow-matching TTS architecture; our findings are demonstrated on dots.tts and **may be architecture-specific** pending replication (e.g., VoxCPM). All code, preference-pair construction, the diagnostic probe, and evaluation pipelines are released under Apache-2.0.

## 1. Introduction

Speech synthesis has long been dominated by two paradigms: end-to-end acoustic models (e.g., FastSpeech2, VITS) and, more recently, discrete codec language models that autoregressively generate quantized audio tokens (VALL-E, Wang et al., 2023; SoundStorm, Borsos et al., 2023). A third paradigm is now emerging: *continuous-latent* AR TTS, which retains the expressive power of autoregressive generation while bypassing the quantization bottleneck entirely. dots.tts (rednote-hilab & SJTU X-LANCE, 2026) is the clearest embodiment of this approach: a Qwen2.5-1.5B language-model backbone emits patch-level hidden states that condition a DiT flow-matching head, which denoises continuous AudioVAE latents at 48 kHz — no discrete audio token appears anywhere in the pipeline. On robustness benchmarks (seed-tts-eval, Anastassiou et al., 2024), dots.tts matches or outperforms codec-LM systems at higher fidelity, and its SOAR post-training further strengthens robustness through an unpublished self-correction procedure.

Despite these advances, continuous-latent TTS faces two alignment challenges that the literature has not addressed. **First**, DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) has become the dominant alignment tool for language models, but it relies on token-level sequence log-probabilities — a quantity that is structurally unavailable in systems whose acoustic output is sampled from a continuous stochastic ODE. To our knowledge, no published recipe instantiates DPO on a continuous-latent AR TTS model. **Second**, *reward hacking* — a model optimized for a single automatic metric (e.g., word error rate, WER) attaining that metric at the cost of naturalness, speaker fidelity, or expressiveness — is widely acknowledged in TTS practitioner discourse but has not been demonstrated in a controlled, reproducible setup with held-out naturalness measures.

Existing TTS post-training efforts close neither gap. REINFORCE-based methods for codec-LM TTS (Seed-TTS, Anastassiou et al., 2024) operate only in discrete token spaces; DPO/GSPO adaptations for TTS (Qwen3-TTS) likewise assume exact token log-probabilities. For continuous-latent architectures, the closest precedent is Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2023), which shows a diffusion model's training loss is a valid log-likelihood surrogate — but this insight has not been adapted to the patch-AR setting of flow-matching TTS with a separate language-model backbone. On the self-correction side, dots.tts's SOAR *weights* are released, yet the training procedure is entirely undocumented, making SOAR non-reproducible and impossible to isolate as an experimental variable.

**In one sentence:** we develop the *natural* adaptation of DPO to continuous-latent flow-matching AR TTS (instantiated on dots.tts), then show — via a direct diagnostic probe — that on this model an automatic-metric reward preference is *anti-correlated* with the model's own flow likelihood, so that likelihood-based DPO becomes the wrong tool: not because $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ fails as a likelihood proxy (it does not — a likelihood-aligned preference optimizes it to saturation), but because the best-vs-worst reward selects the *off-manifold* member of each pair. The paper is therefore a cautionary, diagnostic case study — demonstrated on dots.tts and possibly architecture-specific — of a known likelihood-vs-quality / classifier-free-guidance gap surfacing in continuous-latent TTS alignment, what that implies, and which alignment routes (likelihood-aligned or non-likelihood) escape it.

We make five contributions; together they reframe the work from a "working recipe" into a cautionary, diagnostic study of when likelihood-based DPO is the wrong tool for continuous-latent flow-matching TTS.

1. **DPO-FM: the natural DPO adaptation for continuous-latent flow-matching, and a test of its premise.** Adapting Diffusion-DPO, we derive a DPO objective for continuous-latent AR TTS that substitutes the flow-matching velocity-MSE loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ as an implicit likelihood surrogate, using exactly four forward passes per pair — policy/reference × winner/loser — that share a single noise/timestep draw $(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$ to keep comparisons valid, with an anchor SFT term on winners to prevent drift of the text-conditioning and end-of-sequence heads. To our knowledge this is **among the first** public preference-alignment implementations for any continuous-latent flow-matching TTS architecture; rather than presenting it as a finished recipe, we subject its load-bearing premise — that an automatic-metric reward preference *agrees* with $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ — to a direct empirical test.

2. **An automatic-metric reward preference is anti-correlated with the model's flow likelihood (Spearman $\rho=-0.187$, $N=188$): a likelihood-vs-quality gap, domain-quantified.** A training-free probe (`alignment/probe_fm_reward.py`) over the **full set of $N=188$ preference pairs** shows that the candidate the automatic-metric reward prefers tends to carry the *higher* $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$. The robust, load-bearing fact is a **weak but significant monotonic anti-correlation** between the denoised FM margin and the reward gap: **Spearman $\rho=-0.187$ ($p\approx10^{-2}$; $\rho^2\approx3.5\%$, a weak effect by Cohen's convention)**, with a **Pearson $-0.411$ reported alongside as a leverage-amplified linear coefficient** (its 2.2× larger magnitude is driven by a few high-leverage points; critical $|r|\approx0.143$ at $N=188$, so both clear significance). We lead with the rank statistic and treat the headline as the *monotonic* anti-correlation, sharpest on the largest-quality-gap pairs. The complementary observation that the FM margin is, on average, near-tie (positive on **82/188** of pairs, mean **$-0.0071$**, mean winner win-rate **0.47**; a binomial test against 0.5 gives $p=0.093$ — not significantly different, trending below 0.5) is *not* offered as independent evidence: most of the 188 same-prompt pairs are near-ties, so this average is largely a data-composition artifact, and the diagnosis rests on the anti-correlation (whose survival we therefore also check on the high-gap, non-tie subset). Two safeguards frame the finding. First, an honest revision: an earlier $n=24$ pilot appeared stronger (positive on 4/24, win-rate 0.33, Pearson $-0.496$), but at full scale the apparent *average* winner-dispreference regressed toward chance while the *anti-correlation* persisted and the Spearman coefficient moved from non-significant (at $n=24$) to significant (at $N=188$). Second, a confound check, now decided. Probing the diagnostically correct quantity $\mathrm{corr}(\text{FM-margin},\text{setting-gap})$ (`alignment/probe_analysis.py`) shows num-steps/NFE is a real but mild confounder ($-0.237$, 95% CI $[-0.360,-0.102]$, significant), while guidance and speaker are not ($-0.067$ each, n.s.). Decisively, the **partial correlation** $\mathrm{corr}(\text{FM-margin},\text{reward-gap}\mid\text{guidance},\text{num-steps},\text{speaker})$ is $-0.392$ (95% CI $[-0.548,-0.209]$, significant) — barely changed from the raw Pearson $-0.411$ — so the anti-correlation **survives partialling out the entire sampling grid** (including the one confounded knob) and is **intrinsic** to the preference–likelihood coupling, not a generation-knob artifact. Here "intrinsic" means *not a grid product*, **not** that $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ is a poor likelihood proxy (it is not — open-SOAR optimizes it to saturation); the diagnosis remains preference-likelihood misalignment, i.e., an off-manifold reward winner.

3. **open-SOAR: the one alignment route that survives — and the surprise that SFT does not.** We release **open-SOAR**, a reward-free open approximation of dots.tts's undisclosed SOAR self-correction whose preference is defined by *trajectory cleanliness* (clean target trajectory vs. perturbed-rollout velocity field, clean target retained) rather than an external reward. Because a perturbed rollout *by construction* incurs higher $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$, open-SOAR's preference is aligned *with* the flow likelihood, and its training target is the *real* recording — exactly the configuration the diagnosis predicts should train. The held-out $n{=}100$ results (§5.1) confirm it: **open-SOAR is the only arm that improves** over `base` (best UTMOS, WER ≈ `base`). We had also expected SFT-on-winners — which performs no win/lose $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ contrast — to be safe; it is **not** (WER collapses 0.103→0.545, UTMOS 4.309→3.917), which forces a broader thesis: it is not the DPO contrast specifically but *self-training toward any self-generated, reward-selected off-manifold target* that harms the model. We state this with explicit caveats (§5.1): the SFT collapse is confounded with small-data SFT instability, for which open-SOAR's survival is only a *partial* control.

4. **A controlled reward-hacking comparison — reported as a negative result.** We designed a WER-only arm (`dpo_weronly`) against a multi-objective arm (`dpo_multiobj`: WER + speaker cosine + UTMOS, $z$-normalized per prompt pool, weighted 0.4/0.3/0.3), identical except for the reward. The measured outcome (§5.3) **does not reproduce reward hacking**: the WER-only arm does *not* attain the lowest WER (0.115, above `base` 0.103 and `open_soar` 0.093), so there is no over-optimized-intelligibility solution to trade against naturalness, while the multi-objective arm *collapses* rather than mitigating (WER 0.752, 25% generation failures). Because the mis-specified DPO-FM objective never reaches the low-WER regime, the reward-composition effect is confounded by FM collapse and is *not isolable*; we **downgrade this contribution to a negative finding** and pre-committed to (and here keep) reporting it honestly.

5. **Streaming-first evaluation (planned).** We **will test** whether any offline-alignment gains persist under 1T1A streaming decoding — the production-serving mode — and whether they incur no statistically significant increase in time-to-first-audio (TTFA, i.e., first-chunk latency), to close the gap between offline alignment research and latency-constrained deployment (Table 2, [TBD]).

Experiments use a held-out 100-utterance LJSpeech subset (zero overlap with the 188 preference pairs), seed-tts-eval–style metrics (WER via Whisper-large-v3, speaker similarity via WavLM-SV, UTMOS, EmergentTTS-Eval LLM-judge win-rate), and streaming metrics. Our pre-registered gate — hard-case WER reduction relative to `base` with SIM degradation $\leq 0.01$ and no UTMOS decline — is **decisively failed** by `dpo_multiobj`, which collapses on WER, SIM, and UTMOS with a 25% generation-failure rate (§5.1). Given the intentionally small scale, all deltas carry bootstrap 95% CIs, and we caution against reading absolute scores as cross-paper SOTA. All code, recipes, and evaluation tooling are released under Apache-2.0.

## 2. Related Work

### 2.1 Preference Alignment: RLHF, DPO, and Diffusion-DPO

RLHF (Christiano et al., 2017; Ouyang et al., 2022) established the paradigm of training a reward model on preferences and optimizing the policy via PPO — powerful but expensive and prone to reward over-optimization (Gao et al., 2023), a pathology we revisit as a controlled condition in §5.3. DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) removes the explicit reward model by reparameterizing the RL objective as binary cross-entropy over the policy's log-likelihood ratio between preferred and dispreferred completions. Variants address specific failure modes — IPO (Azar et al., 2024) adds identity regularization; GSPO (Qwen3-TTS) uses group-relative normalization for candidates sampled from the same prompt. **All standard DPO formulations assume tractable per-sequence log-likelihoods**, an assumption that fails for continuous-latent generative models with no discrete tokens and no closed-form $\log\pi(y\mid x)$.

Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2023) solves the analogous problem for image diffusion: since the denoising loss is a variational bound on $-\log p$, it serves as a principled log-likelihood surrogate, and a *shared* noise sample and timestep across the four forward passes ensure comparability. We adopt both the surrogate-loss insight and the shared-RNG constraint, transplanting them from image diffusion to **flow-matching continuous-latent AR speech synthesis**. Three non-trivial adaptations are required: the reference is a frozen AR-model copy rather than a fixed noise prior; the surrogate is velocity-MSE from a DiT head over AudioVAE latents rather than pixel-space denoising loss; and an anchor SFT term on winners prevents drift in the LLM backbone's text conditioning — a component absent in image alignment, where no language model is involved. Critically, all three adaptations *preserve* Diffusion-DPO's load-bearing premise — that a quality preference *agrees* with the likelihood the surrogate proxies — yet none of them *guarantees* it. The decoupling of likelihood from perceptual quality is in fact well established: likelihood is a poor proxy for sample quality in generative models (Theis et al., 2016), and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) deliberately *trades* likelihood for fidelity by pushing samples off the model's manifold — precisely the regime our candidate pool exercises. In image diffusion the denoising loss is computed in the same pixel space a human perceives, so lower loss plausibly means a better picture; in continuous-latent TTS the surrogate instead lives in an AudioVAE latent decoded by a separate flow head, and the quality reward is an automatic-metric score, so whether the reward preference agrees with $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ is an empirical question rather than an inherited guarantee. This is precisely the premise we test directly in §6.7, and where — on dots.tts — the reward preference and the likelihood turn out to *disagree*.

### 2.2 Post-Training for TTS: REINFORCE, DPO/GSPO, and Self-Correction

Three post-training paradigms have emerged for TTS, with no controlled comparison among them in the literature.

**REINFORCE with task rewards.** Seed-TTS (Anastassiou et al., 2024) is the first TTS system to report RL post-training: a WER-based REINFORCE reward sharply reduces error on hard utterances. The authors themselves note that optimizing solely for WER **degrades naturalness and speaker similarity** — a reward-hacking effect flagged but not systematically characterized. We turn this anecdote into a controlled experiment (`dpo_weronly`) and **plan to test** whether the degradation replicates under UTMOS and EmergentTTS win-rate, quantifying the WER-only vs. multi-objective gap (Table 3, [TBD]).

**DPO/GSPO on discrete codec-LMs.** Qwen3-TTS applies DPO and GSPO to a codec-LM that tokenizes audio into discrete AR tokens; exact token log-probabilities make standard DPO directly applicable. This is **architecturally incompatible** with continuous-latent systems: dots.tts produces no discrete tokens and its flow-matching head has no token-level log-prob. Our velocity-MSE surrogate is precisely the bridge such systems do not need and therefore do not provide.

**Reward-free self-correction.** The original SOAR mechanism in dots.tts takes a third path with no external reward: preference pairs contrast clean target trajectories (winners) against perturbed-rollout velocity fields (losers), training the flow-matching head to correct back toward clean targets. SOAR improves word-level faithfulness; however, code analysis (our supplementary §A) confirms **the training procedure is not open-sourced** — only the fine-tuned weights are released — so one cannot verify the pairing strategy, isolate SOAR against a matched SFT baseline, or extend it. Our **open-SOAR** re-implementation addresses all three within the same DPO-FM framework, enabling a principled treatment-vs-treatment comparison (open-SOAR vs. `dpo_multiobj`) that disentangles self-correction from external preference. We label open-SOAR explicitly as an *approximation*, not a faithful reproduction, and treat the attribution gap as a stated limitation.

Robustness improvements short of preference learning also exist: VALL-E 2 (Chen et al., 2024) introduces Repetition-Aware Sampling, eliminating degenerate looping in AR codec-LMs at sampling time with no extra training — but leaves hard-case WER and the naturalness–robustness trade-off (our two target axes) unresolved.

### 2.3 Continuous-Latent TTS Without Discrete Audio Tokens

The dominant 2024–2026 TTS architecture pairs an AR LM for token prediction with a discrete codec (RVQ) and a separate acoustic decoder (VALL-E; Seed-TTS; Qwen3-TTS). Discretization imposes quantization loss, expands sequence length, and makes accumulated AR errors hard to correct downstream.

**Non-autoregressive continuous-latent systems.** NaturalSpeech 2 (Shen et al., 2023) replaces discrete tokens with latent diffusion over continuous RVQ latents; NaturalSpeech 3 (Ju et al., 2024) adds a factorized latent space (FACodec) for attribute-disentangled control. Both are **fully non-autoregressive (NAR)** and lack the LLM backbone that provides long-range contextual coherence and is the direct target of our DPO loss. Voicebox (Le et al., 2023) and F5-TTS (Chen et al., 2024) demonstrate flow-matching / CFM as competitive NAR TTS without discrete tokens, and are direct antecedents of dots.tts's flow-matching component; but being NAR they lack the AR hallucination pathology that motivates self-correction, and **neither has been paired with preference learning**. Ours is, to our knowledge, **among the first** DPO-style objectives applied to a flow-matching TTS backbone of any kind.

**Continuous-latent AR systems.** dots.tts pairs a Qwen2.5-1.5B LLM backbone with a DiT flow-matching head that autoregressively predicts patch-level continuous AudioVAE latents at 48 kHz — no discrete audio tokens at any stage — while enabling 1T1A interleaved streaming, under Apache-2.0 (code and weights). **This is the model we target**, using its exposed `fm_loss` LossTerm as the DPO surrogate. VoxCPM (OpenBMB, 2026) independently validates the continuous-latent AR paradigm from a second group via a tokenizer-free diffusion-AR pipeline on a MiniCPM-4 backbone, strengthening the case that continuous-latent AR generalizes. We target dots.tts because its DiT head exposes velocity-MSE directly in training code, giving the cleanest instantiation of the Diffusion-DPO surrogate; extending to VoxCPM's diffusion-AR route is future work.

**Delta.** Across §2.1–§2.3, no prior work brings preference optimization to a continuous-latent AR generative model that lacks discrete-token log-probs. Our contribution is threefold and deliberately scoped: (1) **among the first** DPO-style instantiations on such an architecture (DPO-FM on dots.tts), handling the absence of token log-probs via the flow-matching likelihood surrogate; (2) a **domain quantification**, on this architecture, of the known likelihood-vs-quality / CFG off-manifold gap (Theis et al., 2016; Ho & Salimans, 2022) — that an automatic-metric reward preference can run counter to the model's own flow likelihood — which we localize as a property of the *preference–likelihood coupling* rather than of the surrogate as a likelihood estimator; and (3) **open-SOAR**, a reward-free, likelihood-aligned self-correction route, alongside a *planned* controlled study of multi-objective reward trade-offs and reward hacking. We demonstrate all three on a fully open-source system (dots.tts), and flag that the diagnosis may be architecture-specific pending replication on a second continuous-latent AR model such as VoxCPM.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Background: dots.tts as a Continuous-Latent AR TTS

dots.tts departs from the discrete codec-LM paradigm by operating entirely in a continuous latent space. *(Architecture figures below are stated to the granularity needed for the method; the precise layer/dimension counts should be cross-checked against the dots.tts technical report before camera-ready.)*

**AudioVAE.** A pre-trained, frozen AudioVAE encodes 48 kHz waveforms into continuous latent vectors at a low frame rate, decoded back to audio by a causal convolutional decoder. No codebook or vector quantization is used: there are **no discrete audio tokens**.

**Patch-AR backbone.** A causal Transformer *semantic encoder* compresses each VAE-latent patch into a single token, fed autoregressively into a **Qwen2.5-1.5B** LLM backbone. Speaker identity is injected through a frozen x-vector extractor. The backbone produces hidden states that condition the acoustic head.

**Flow-matching DiT head.** A DiT acoustic head generates the continuous VAE latent via flow-matching. Given target latent $\mathbf{x}_1=\mathrm{AudioVAE}(\text{target audio})$ and noise $\mathbf{x}_0\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})$, the interpolant at time $t\in[0,1]$ is $\mathbf{x}_t=(1-t)\mathbf{x}_0+t\mathbf{x}_1$ with conditional velocity target ($\sigma=0$) $u_t=\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_0$. The DiT predicts $u_t$ from $(\mathbf{x}_t, t, \mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{cond}})$, where $\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{cond}}$ encodes the LLM hidden state and speaker x-vector. The training loss is the per-patch velocity MSE averaged over the audio span:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}(\theta;\mathbf{x}_1)=\mathbb{E}_{t,\mathbf{x}_0}\!\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t\mid\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{cond}})-u_t\right\|^2\right]\tag{1}$$

Because the audio pathway never passes through a categorical distribution, dots.tts has **no token log-probabilities** — the root obstacle for standard preference alignment.

### 3.2 DPO-FM: DPO over Flow-Matching

**Why standard DPO is inapplicable.** The DPO objective (Rafailov et al., 2023) is

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}=-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y^w,y^l)}\!\left[\log\sigma\!\left(\beta\Big(\log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y^w|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^w|x)}-\log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y^l|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^l|x)}\Big)\right)\right]\tag{2}$$

which requires summing token log-probabilities over a discrete sequence. In dots.tts the output is a continuous flow trajectory; no such distribution exists.

**Velocity-MSE as a heuristic implicit-likelihood proxy.** The conditional flow-matching loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ is, by construction, an unbiased estimator of the (otherwise intractable) marginal vector-field MSE (Lipman et al., 2023); it is **not** a proven variational upper bound on $-\log p(\mathbf{x}_1)$ in the way a diffusion ELBO is. Adapting Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2023), we therefore treat $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ as a **heuristic** implicit-likelihood proxy — a lower $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ is *taken to* indicate higher implicit likelihood — and flag this as a heuristic rather than a guarantee. We define the **implicit log-ratio** of policy over reference for a sample as

$$\Delta(\mathbf{x}_1)\triangleq\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}(\theta_{\mathrm{ref}};\mathbf{x}_1)-\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}(\theta;\mathbf{x}_1)\tag{3}$$

A large positive $\Delta$ indicates the policy reduced velocity error more than the reference, i.e., assigns higher relative implicit likelihood to $\mathbf{x}_1$.

**The DPO-FM objective.** For a preference pair $(\mathbf{x}_1^w,\mathbf{x}_1^l)$ sharing conditioning context $x$ (text + reference speaker audio),

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO\text{-}FM}}=-\log\sigma\!\left(\beta\big(\Delta(\mathbf{x}_1^w)-\Delta(\mathbf{x}_1^l)\big)\right)\tag{4}$$

Substituting Eq. (3) yields the directly implementable velocity-MSE form (`alignment/losses_dpo.py`):

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO\text{-}FM}}=-\log\sigma\!\Bigg(\!-\beta\Big[\big(\underbrace{\|\mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t^w)-u_t^w\|^2}_{\text{policy, win}}-\underbrace{\|\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{x}_t^w)-u_t^w\|^2}_{\text{ref, win}}\big)-\big(\underbrace{\|\mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t^l)-u_t^l\|^2}_{\text{policy, lose}}-\underbrace{\|\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{x}_t^l)-u_t^l\|^2}_{\text{ref, lose}}\big)\Big]\Bigg)\tag{5}$$

Each squared error is averaged over the audio span's patch mask, exactly replicating dots.tts's `_compute_fm_loss_term`. The reference $\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is a copy of the base checkpoint, **frozen throughout training**.

**Anchor SFT regularizer.** DPO-FM acts on the DiT head. To prevent drift in the text-conditioning and EOS-prediction pathways of the LLM backbone, we add a cross-entropy regularizer on winners only:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO\text{-}FM}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}\big(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{EOS}}\big)_{\mathbf{x}^w}\tag{6}$$

with $\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}$ selected on a held-out validation set; we ablate this term (§6.1) to confirm its role.

**Correctness invariant: shared $(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$.** Each $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ in Eq. (5) is a Monte Carlo estimate depending on $(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$. If the four forward passes each drew their own $(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$, the difference $\Delta(\mathbf{x}^w)-\Delta(\mathbf{x}^l)$ would conflate the genuine preference signal with spurious Monte Carlo variance — which the optimizer could exploit. We therefore enforce: **within a step, all four passes share one $(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$ draw**; with $\xi=(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$ drawn once,

$$\mathbf{x}_t^w=(1-t)\mathbf{x}_0+t\mathbf{x}_1^w,\;\; u_t^w=\mathbf{x}_1^w-\mathbf{x}_0;\qquad \mathbf{x}_t^l=(1-t)\mathbf{x}_0+t\mathbf{x}_1^l,\;\; u_t^l=\mathbf{x}_1^l-\mathbf{x}_0$$

using the *same* $\mathbf{x}_0$ and $t$. The question is: "for this specific noise/timestep, does the model better predict the clean velocity for the winner or the loser?" — meaningful only when the draw is held fixed (paralleling the shared-$t$ convention of Wallace et al., 2023). **Across steps, $\xi$ is drawn independently** ($\mathbf{x}_0\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})$, $t\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1]$) so the objective marginalizes over all flow phases. We implement this via `torch.random.fork_rng()` with a single per-step RNG state replayed before each pass; without the fix, training loss is noisier by $\sim$__× with slower validation convergence.

**Assumption boundary: does an automatic-metric reward preference agree with $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$?** The entire DPO-FM construction rests on one load-bearing premise inherited from Diffusion-DPO: that a preferred winner (lower WER, higher SIM/UTMOS under our automatic-metric reward) also attains a *lower* $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ than its dispreferred loser, so that the reward preference and the implicit likelihood point the *same* way. If this premise holds, Eq. (5) pushes implicit likelihood toward better-scoring audio. If it fails — if the reward instead prefers the candidate that is *less typical* under the model's own flow head (e.g., a more aggressively guided, off-manifold sample) — then the win/lose $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ contrast points the *wrong way* and likelihood-based DPO is the wrong tool, **even though $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ remains a perfectly serviceable likelihood proxy**: a likelihood-aligned preference still trains on it to saturation (§3.4, §6.7). The failure, in other words, lives in the *coupling* between reward and likelihood, not in the surrogate itself. Critically, this premise is testable *directly and without training*, by measuring the sign of the denoised FM margin and its correlation with the reward gap on held-out preference pairs. We run exactly this probe over the full $N=188$ pairs in **§6.7**, and its outcome is the central cautionary finding of this paper: the premise does **not** hold. The denoised FM margin is **weakly but significantly monotonically anti-correlated** with the reward gap (Spearman $\rho=-0.187$, $\rho^2\approx3.5\%$; Pearson $-0.411$ alongside as a leverage-amplified linear coefficient), sharpest on the largest-quality-gap pairs; on average the margin is near-tie (positive on 82/188 of pairs, win-rate 0.47), but that average is dominated by near-tie pairs and is not the load-bearing fact. We therefore present the objective above as the *natural* continuous-latent adaptation of DPO and then interrogate, rather than assume, its premise.

### 3.3 Multi-Objective Automatic Reward and Preference-Pair Construction

**Candidate generation.** For each conditioning prompt $x$ we sample $K$ candidates $\{\mathbf{x}_1^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^K$ from the frozen base model by sweeping the inference grid implemented in `candidates/generate_candidates.py` (`DEFAULT_GRID`): guidance scale $g\in\{1.0,1.2,1.6\}$, flow-matching function evaluations NFE (num-steps) $\in\{8,10,16\}$, and speaker scale $\in\{1.2,1.5\}$ (default $K\in\{4,6,8\}$). The released grid varies *only* these three knobs — there is no temperature axis. This grid-induced diversity yields candidates with meaningfully different WER/SIM/naturalness profiles without any gradient update during data collection, avoiding on-policy feedback instability.

**Reward signals.** Each candidate is scored on three automatic axes: $r_{\mathrm{WER}}$ (Whisper-large-v3 WER, lower⇒better), $r_{\mathrm{SIM}}$ (WavLM-SV cosine to the reference, higher⇒better), $r_{\mathrm{UTMOS}}$ (UTMOS MOS predictor, higher⇒better).

**Per-prompt $z$-normalization.** We normalize each dimension *within* the $K$-candidate pool of the same prompt before aggregation:

$$\hat r_{\mathrm{dim}}^{(k)}=\frac{r_{\mathrm{dim}}^{(k)}-\mu_{\mathrm{dim}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{dim}}+\epsilon},\quad \mu_{\mathrm{dim}},\sigma_{\mathrm{dim}}\text{ over }\{r_{\mathrm{dim}}^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^K\tag{7}$$

This removes between-prompt scale differences (an inherently hard text has universally high WER) and removes the cross-axis scale mismatch between WER (0–100) and cosine (0–1), so the signal reflects *relative* in-pool quality. The composite reward is

$$r^{(k)}=w_{\mathrm{WER}}\hat r_{\mathrm{WER}}^{(k)}+w_{\mathrm{SIM}}\hat r_{\mathrm{SIM}}^{(k)}+w_{\mathrm{UTMOS}}\hat r_{\mathrm{UTMOS}}^{(k)}\tag{8}$$

with default $(w_{\mathrm{WER}},w_{\mathrm{SIM}},w_{\mathrm{UTMOS}})=(0.4,0.3,0.3)$ for `dpo_multiobj` (ablated in §6.1).

**Pair selection.** We adopt a **best-vs-worst** strategy: $\mathbf{x}_1^w=\arg\max_k r^{(k)}$, $\mathbf{x}_1^l=\arg\min_k r^{(k)}$. Maximizing the margin gives the clearest preference signal, important in our small-data regime (alternatives `top2_bottom2` and threshold-$\tau$ are ablated in §6.1).

**The `dpo_weronly` condition.** We additionally train with $(1.0, 0.0, 0.0)$ as a **deliberate experimental arm** to probe whether a WER-only signal over-optimizes intelligibility at the expense of naturalness and expressiveness. Its design rationale is detailed here; its outcome is analyzed as a primary result in §5.3 and §6.4. The multi-objective formulation (Eq. 8) is the proposed mitigation, with the SIM and UTMOS terms acting as regularizers against naturalness collapse.

### 3.4 open-SOAR: Reward-Free Self-Correction

The original SOAR procedure for `dots.tts-soar` improves robustness via reward-free self-correction on the DiT head, but only the trained checkpoint is public; the algorithm, data, and hyperparameters are closed (the repository contains no SOAR training code). **open-SOAR** is an open approximation using the same DPO-FM framework with no external reward.

For a prompt $x$ with ground-truth target latent $\mathbf{x}_1$, draw clean noise $\mathbf{x}_0\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})$ and a perturbation $\boldsymbol{\eta}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma_{\mathrm{soar}}^2\mathbf{I})$.

- **Winner (on-trajectory):** $\mathbf{x}_t^w=(1-t)\mathbf{x}_0+t\mathbf{x}_1$, target $u_t^w=\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_0$ — the well-conditioned trajectory.
- **Loser (off-trajectory):** perturbed start $\mathbf{x}_0'=\mathbf{x}_0+\boldsymbol{\eta}$, giving $\mathbf{x}_t^l=(1-t)\mathbf{x}_0'+t\mathbf{x}_1$, with the target **kept identical to the winner's clean target**: $u_t^l=\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_0$ (not $\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_0'$).

Because the loser starts from a perturbed position but the target still points toward $\mathbf{x}_1$ via the *clean* displacement, the model incurs systematically higher error at $\mathbf{x}_t^l$ until it learns to self-correct. Applying $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO\text{-}FM}}$ on these pairs trains the model to predict the correct velocity even when the starting position deviates — the self-correction capacity we seek. The shared-$(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$ invariant from §3.2 still holds: winner and loser share the same drawn $t$ and base $\mathbf{x}_0$; only the loser's input interpolant is perturbed by $\boldsymbol{\eta}$.

Because open-SOAR needs no external reward, it trains on data with no ASR/SIM scores, and the contrast `open_soar` vs. `dpo_multiobj` is designed to isolate RQ4: any change in `open_soar` relative to `base` is attributable to self-correction, and any *additional* change in `dpo_multiobj` to the external multi-objective preference. In the event (§5.1) this contrast is asymmetric — `open_soar` is the only arm that does not degrade, whereas `dpo_multiobj` collapses — so RQ4 resolves not into "how much extra the external preference adds" but into "why training toward reward-selected self-generated winners is harmful while self-correction is not." Both arms train from the same `base` checkpoint.

> **Naming and provenance.** open-SOAR is an open-source *approximation* of the original SOAR procedure, not a re-implementation. In results tables, `dots.tts-soar` (official weights) is reported separately as a non-reproducible upper-bound reference and does not participate in same-condition comparisons.

## 4. Experimental Setup

We organize the study around four research questions. **RQ1 (primary):** Does multi-objective DPO-FM improve robustness (hard-case WER, UTMOS) over `base` without degrading SIM/naturalness? **RQ2:** Do gains transfer to 1T1A streaming without hurting TTFA? **RQ3:** Does WER-only reward induce reward hacking, and does multi-objective reward mitigate it? **RQ4:** How much robustness comes from reward-free self-correction (open-SOAR) vs. external multi-objective preference (DPO-FM)?

### 4.1 Data

**Preference pairs.** We generate self-sampled candidates from `dots.tts-base` on LJSpeech prompts (single-speaker English, resampled to 48 kHz), varying the inference grid of §3.3 to induce diversity. Multi-objective rewards are computed per candidate, $z$-normalized per prompt (Eq. 7), and aggregated (Eq. 8); `best_worst` pairing yields **188 preference pairs**. This deliberately small scale is consistent with the paper's goal of a *reproducible recipe* rather than absolute SOTA; all claims are stated as deltas relative to `base`, never as comparisons to externally reported numbers. *(The released codebase implements a broader multilingual, multi-speaker protocol — VCTK + AISHELL-3 + LJSpeech, seed-tts-eval `test-zh`/`test-zh-hard` — for scale-up; reported results here are the single-speaker English pilot. See §7 and the Reviewer self-check.)*

**Evaluation set.** A held-out 100-sentence LJSpeech subset, strictly non-overlapping with training prompts (verified by transcript-level exact matching). Robustness is measured by WER and a **hard-case WER** stratum (robustness-stressing utterances: long, numeric, repetition-prone). Expressiveness uses EmergentTTS-Eval with LLM-judge pairwise scoring (head-to-head vs. `base`; win-rate annotated as a non-absolute score).

**Licenses and preprocessing.** LJSpeech is used under its original redistribution terms (per-corpus details in `data/LICENSES.md`; items marked `[需核实]` confirmed before the final run). Audio is resampled to 48 kHz (dots.tts native) with SoX sinc anti-aliasing.

### 4.2 Treatment Conditions

All arms share the backbone (`dots.tts-base`: Qwen2.5-1.5B + DiT flow-matching head + frozen AudioVAE), train from the same checkpoint, and use the same prompt split (Table 1).

| ID | Description | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `base` | dots.tts-base, no fine-tuning | Lower bound |
| `sft` | SFT on winner utterances only | Controls for "more good data" |
| `open_soar` | Reward-free self-correction DPO-FM (§3.4) | Isolates self-correction (RQ4) |
| `dpo_multiobj` | DPO-FM, reward = WER + SIM + UTMOS (0.4/0.3/0.3) | **Primary method** (RQ1) |
| `dpo_weronly` | DPO-FM, reward = WER only | Controlled reward-hacking probe (RQ3) |

The `sft` arm is critical as a control: it isolates whether merely fine-tuning on the (reward-selected) winner utterances — without any contrastive preference signal — is benign. Contrary to our initial expectation that it would be the safe baseline, `sft` itself collapses (§5.1), which reframes the comparison: the relevant axis is not "contrast vs. no-contrast" but *what the model is trained toward* (reward-selected self-generated winners, which harm both `sft` and `dpo_multiobj`, vs. the clean target of `open_soar`). The official `dots.tts-soar` weights appear as an upper-bound reference but are excluded from significance testing (training procedure not public).

### 4.3 Metrics

Reported in two modes (offline / 1T1A streaming). **WER:** seed-tts-eval pipeline with Whisper-large-v3; both standard and **hard-case WER** (the primary robustness target; RQ1 gate). **SIM:** WavLM-SV cosine to the reference (seed-tts-eval speaker encoder); secondary gate (`dpo_multiobj` must not degrade SIM by more than $\Delta=0.01$ vs. `base`). **UTMOS:** naturalness MOS via `speechmos`; must be non-degraded for the gate. **Expressiveness:** EmergentTTS-Eval pairwise LLM-judge win-rate vs. `base`; we randomize left/right order, report win-rate as non-absolute, and disclose the judge identity in Reproducibility. **Streaming:** TTFA (ms, first-chunk latency), real-time factor (RTF), chunk-boundary discontinuity (normalized L2 in AudioVAE latent space between adjacent chunks, via `eval/streaming_metrics.py`), and streaming WER. All streaming measurements use the `DoubleStreamingSession` 1T1A configuration.

### 4.4 Evaluation Protocol

(1) All treatments are evaluated on the identical held-out set with the same ASR and speaker-encoder backends. (2) Our numbers are **not** compared to externally reported figures (differing encoders/ASR/subsets make cross-paper numeric comparison invalid). (3) EmergentTTS win-rates are annotated "non-absolute" and never the sole basis for a claim. (4) The official `dots.tts-soar` row is typeset in italics in Table 1 to mark it as a non-reproducible reference.

### 4.5 Implementation Details

**DPO-FM training.** The objective (Eq. 5) is implemented in `alignment/losses_dpo.py`. The four passes per step share `x0` and `t`, seeded via `torch.random.fork_rng()` to guarantee comparability. The anchor SFT regularizer ($\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}=0.2$, anchoring the winner's FM acoustic loss; see §6.2) is applied on winners. We train with $\beta=0.5$ for $600$ steps for all DPO variants ($\beta$/anchor sensitivity in §6.2). Full-parameter fine-tuning on 2×H100 (80 GB); LoRA (rank $16$, $\alpha=32$) on a single A6000 (48 GB) as a compute-efficient fallback for ablation sweeps. **Reward computation:** Whisper-large-v3 (WER), WavLM-SV (SIM), `speechmos` (UTMOS); all $z$-normalized per prompt before weighting. **open-SOAR:** winners from the clean target trajectory and losers from a perturbed rollout (`soar_noise_scale` $=0.5$) with identical target $u_t$; no reward model; otherwise identical to `dpo_multiobj` except for pair construction.

### 4.6 Statistical Methodology

Given the small held-out set (100 sentences) and modest pair budget (188), we avoid asymptotic tests. All point estimates carry **bootstrap 95% CIs** (B = 10,000 utterance-level resamples). Wide CIs — expected at this scale — are reported as-is and discussed in §7. A claim is supported only when the CI of the relevant delta excludes zero; CI-crosses-zero cases are described as inconclusive.

**Reading — one arm improves, the rest collapse, and the collapse is monotone.** The measured $n=100$ results overturn the pre-registered RQ1 expectation and, more consequentially, overturn this paper's own earlier reading that *both* `open_soar` and `sft` are "safe" constructive arms.

- **`open_soar` is the only arm that improves.** It attains the best UTMOS in the table (4.408 [4.386, 4.432], above `base`'s 4.309 [4.273, 4.347]) and the lowest WER (0.093 [0.070, 0.121] vs. `base` 0.103), at the cost of a modest SIM drop (0.844 [0.831, 0.860] vs. `base` 0.891). It is the single arm whose CIs are consistent with a real, non-degrading gain.
- **`dpo_weronly` roughly holds.** WER 0.115 [0.087, 0.145] ≈ `base`, SIM 0.852 essentially unchanged, UTMOS 4.112 [4.050, 4.172] slightly *below* `base` — neither hacking nor improving (analyzed in §5.3).
- **`sft` collapses.** WER explodes to 0.545 [0.251, 1.059], SIM falls to 0.673 [0.605, 0.741], UTMOS drops to 3.917 [3.758, 4.064]. This **refutes the hypothesis — held in our own earlier §6.7 framing and singled out by Reviewer 3 as the "truly unaffected control" — that SFT-on-winners sidesteps the failure.** It does not; it is the second-worst arm. (The $n=3$ smoke test that earlier made `sft` look best, WER 0.039, drew all three prompts from the `LJ001` prefix and entirely missed the `LJ002` collapse; see §7.)
- **`dpo_multiobj` is a disaster.** Among the 75 prompts it could synthesize at all, WER 0.752 [0.566, 1.035], SIM 0.365 [0.280, 0.460], UTMOS 2.841 [2.583, 3.105] — every axis collapsed — and on the remaining 25% it produced no audio (immediate-EOS). Its WER CI [0.566, 1.035] does **not overlap** `base`'s [0.071, 0.142].

**The collapse is monotone in how hard each arm is pulled toward self-generated, automatic-reward-selected winners.** Order the arms by the strength with which training pulls the model toward its *own* reward-selected candidate output (rather than toward real recorded audio): `open_soar` (no reward; pulled toward the **real clean target trajectory**) → `dpo_weronly` (weak DPO pull toward a WER-only winner; ~63% of pairs near-tie) → `sft` (strong supervised pull toward the reward-selected winner) → `dpo_multiobj` (strong DPO pull toward the *cleanest* multi-objective reward-winner). Along exactly this ordering the outcome degrades monotonically: improvement → ≈ `base` → collapse → catastrophic collapse + a 25% immediate-EOS generation-failure rate (a generation-time collapse on a prompt subset; whether it is graded degradation or optimization divergence is left open — caveat 2). The one arm trained toward a *real, on-manifold* target (`open_soar`) is the one that improves; every arm trained toward *self-generated, reward-selected* targets degrades, and the harder the pull the worse the damage.

**What this implies (a cautionary hypothesis, with the caveats below).** The §6.7 diagnosis — that an automatic-metric reward preference anti-correlates with the model's own flow likelihood — was framed as a property of the DPO-FM *win/lose contrast over* $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$. The $n=100$ results suggest something broader and more cautionary: **self-training on automatic-reward-selected, self-generated candidates degrades the model when those candidates are off-manifold, and this holds regardless of whether the training objective is DPO or plain SFT.** Best-vs-worst reward selection over a guidance-diverse candidate pool tends to pick the clear-but-atypical (high-guidance, off-manifold) member; pulling the model toward those self-generated off-manifold winners — by any objective — pulls it off its own manifold. Only `open_soar`, which trains toward the genuine recorded target, escapes. This narrows the constructive conclusion from "`open_soar` *and* `sft` are safe" to "**only `open_soar` is safe; `sft` collapses too.**"

**Caveats (load-bearing; the magnitudes above are directional only).**
1. **The `sft` collapse is confounded with ordinary small-data SFT pathology.** Fine-tuning a 1.5B backbone on 188 single-speaker pairs can overfit / catastrophically forget on its own, independent of *which* targets are used. We cannot cleanly separate "off-manifold target" from "SFT is simply unstable at this scale" without an `SFT-on-random-candidates` vs. `SFT-on-reward-winners` control (future work). What we *can* say is that `open_soar` — a fine-tune of the *same scale* and the *same optimizer family* — does **not** collapse, which is a **partial** control pointing at the *target distribution* (self-generated off-manifold winner vs. real target) rather than at "SFT-at-this-scale" alone.
2. **The `dpo_multiobj` 25% failure is a generation-time collapse on a prompt subset; whether it is graded degradation or optimization divergence is, on present evidence, mixed and inconclusive.** The 25% immediate-EOS / empty-payload rate (a collapse of the EOS/length head at generation time) is far more severe than the weak monotonic anti-correlation (Spearman $-0.187$, $\rho^2\approx3.5\%$) alone would produce, so we do **not** attribute it to that graded effect alone. But the evidence does **not** cleanly support calling it an optimization *divergence* either, and we no longer assert "divergence, not graded." Pulling against the divergence reading: the available training curve **did not re-diverge** — the winner-side velocity error $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$ stayed stable at $\sim0.74$ throughout (end/early ratio $1.01\times$, no second blow-up) with the reward margin still small ($0.065$), i.e., nothing like the early weak-anchor over-optimization escalation of §6.2. Pulling toward it: a cross-tabulation shows the FM margin **decouples from reward on the largest-gap pairs** (same-direction on only 34% of large-gap pairs vs. 48% of small-gap pairs). The consistent reading is therefore *not* a training-time divergence (no run0-style blow-up) but a **mild training trajectory that nonetheless drifts the model off-manifold, so that generation collapses on a subset of prompts** (the 25% immediate-EOS). We report this conservatively and flag a manual TensorBoard-curve inspection plus a listening adjudication as **pending**. If anything this **strengthens** the off-manifold thesis of §6.7: a wrong-direction gradient need not blow up the training metrics to do damage — it can quietly push the velocity field off the model's own manifold, so the failure surfaces only at generation time and only on part of the prompt distribution.
3. **The failures concentrate in the `LJ002-xxxx` block, but that block is most of the eval set.** Both `sft` and `dpo_multiobj` degrade specifically on this segment (and `dpo_multiobj`'s 25 generation failures all fall in it); however, the held-out set (the first 100 held-out prompts, `eval_big`) is **89% `LJ002` (89/100) vs. only 11% `LJ001` (11/100)**, so "all in `LJ002`" is *partly* a composition artifact. The residual, genuine signal is the **within-block rate gap**: `LJ001` 0/11 = 0% vs. `LJ002` 25/89 = 28% (small-$n$, $p\approx0.02$) — a real prompt-dependence. The failing prompts are only **mildly longer** than the pool (mean text length 105 vs. 94 chars, $1.12\times$) and show **no position bias** (52% in the latter half), so we **do not** frame this as "long prompts trigger the collapse"; the trigger is a prompt-subset dependence we have not yet pinned to a single structural factor. Because the collapse is non-uniform, the table's pooled CIs mix a possibly-healthy `LJ001` region with a collapsed `LJ002` region.
4. **CIs on the collapsed arms are enormous, the corpus is single-speaker LJSpeech, and there is no human listening test.** `sft`'s WER CI alone spans [0.251, 1.059]. All collapse magnitudes are *directional* evidence of failure, not precise effect sizes, and "naturalness" rests on UTMOS (an automatic, out-of-domain proxy), not human MOS.
5. **`dpo_multiobj`'s row reflects only the 75 prompts it could synthesize.** The 25 it could not are, by construction, worse than anything counted; the 25% failure rate is therefore an inseparable part of the result and is reported beside — never instead of — the WER/SIM/UTMOS.

**Gate and RQ verdicts.** The primary RQ1 gate (hard-WER reduction vs. `base`, SIM degradation $\leq 0.01$, no UTMOS decline) is **decisively failed by `dpo_multiobj`** on every axis we measured (overall WER, SIM, and UTMOS all collapse, plus a 25% generation-failure rate), so its non-satisfaction does not depend on the un-computed hard-WER stratum. ### 6.2 DPO $\beta$ and Anchor Sensitivity: A Narrow Stability Window

DPO-FM's training dynamics are governed *jointly* by the preference temperature $\beta$ (Eq. 5) and the anchor-SFT weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}$ (Eq. 6). Tuning them on the pilot corpus (188 pairs, 2×H100) exposed a **narrow stability window**: outside it the objective degenerates in one of two opposite directions, and even inside it the attainable preference signal is capped by the data. The phenomena below are qualitative training-dynamics observations from pilot runs — reward margin, the DPO-FM loss, and the per-side winner/loser velocity errors $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$/$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{l}$ (logged in `docs/RUN_JOURNAL.md`); the held-out benchmark that validates the chosen operating point is Tables 1–3 (TBD).

**Anchor too weak → degenerate over-optimization.** If the anchor regularizes only the LLM-backbone heads (text cross-entropy and EOS), it is effectively inert under TTS conditioning, where the transcript is a fixed condition so $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}\!\approx\!0$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{EOS}}\!\approx\!0.05$, leaving the winner's *acoustic* FM loss free to drift. In this regime ($\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}\!=\!0.1$, $\beta\!=\!0.1$) the reward margin inflated to $\approx\!93$–$170$ and the winner velocity error $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$ escalated from $\sim$1 to $\approx\!11$–$86$, while reward accuracy pinned at $1.0$ — a **deceptive** signal: the optimizer maximizes the margin not by improving winners but by degrading losers (and pulling winners along with them), so rank-accuracy reads perfect precisely while acoustic quality collapses. This is the global over-optimization failure recorded in our run journal, and it is the direct reason Eq. (6)'s anchor must regularize the winner's FM acoustic loss rather than the near-zero CE/EOS terms.

**Anchor too strong → preference signal suppressed.** Re-anchoring on $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$ removes this escalation ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$ returns to a healthy $\sim$0.55), but at a high anchor weight ($\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}\!=\!0.5$) it over-corrects into the opposite failure. Holding $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$ at its supervised optimum also holds $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{l}$ down, because winner and loser are candidates for the *same* utterance and are acoustically similar; with $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}\!\approx\!\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{l}$ the margin collapses to $\sim$0.01 and the DPO-FM loss sits at its no-preference floor $\ln 2\!\approx\!0.693$ — the objective has quietly degenerated into SFT-on-winners. Relaxing to $\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}\!=\!0.2$ at intermediate $\beta\!=\!0.3$ keeps $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$ healthy (0.6–1.0) yet leaves the margin at $\sim$0.01: the signal is no longer suppressed by the anchor, but still does not separate.

**Inside the window, the margin is data-bounded.** At $\beta\!=\!0.5$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}\!=\!0.2$ the objective finally separates — but only **intermittently**. Most steps stay flat (margin $\sim$0.01) while a minority open a real margin by raising the loser's error with the winner held near its anchor (e.g., one pilot step reached margin $0.778$ at $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{l}\!=\!1.45$ vs. $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}\!=\!0.75$; another, $0.478$ at $1.12$ vs. $0.62$). The diagnosis is that $\beta\!=\!0.5$ is large enough to split pairs carrying a *genuine* acoustic quality gap without breaking the anchor, and that the low *average* margin reflects the corpus composition rather than a hyperparameter fault: most of the 188 same-prompt pairs are near-ties (their candidates are acoustically too alike). The achievable margin is thus upper-bounded by the data, not by $\beta$ — a constraint we elevate to a first-class limitation in §7.

**Operating point and open status.** We retain $\beta\!=\!0.5$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}\!=\!0.2$ as the pilot operating point (shipped in `configs/dpo_multiobj_fullparam.yaml`, `dpo_weronly.yaml`, and `open_soar_selfcorrect.yaml`: `beta: 0.5`, `lambda_sft: 0.2`, `max_train_steps: 600`). Two lessons generalize beyond these values: (i) **anchor and monitor the *absolute* acoustic quality** $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$ — relative diagnostics (reward accuracy, margin) are insufficient and can actively mislead, since reward accuracy $=1.0$ masked a total acoustic collapse; and (ii) the usable band in $(\beta,\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}})$ is narrow, so the two must be tuned *jointly*, not in isolation. We stress this is an operating point established from training dynamics, **not** a resolved optimum: closing the low average-margin gap is unfinished work (§7), and the planned held-out $\beta$ sweep over $\{0.1,0.5,2.0\}$ (Fig. 3, Table 4) validating the choice on the final WER/SIM/UTMOS axes is TBD.

### 6.4 Why Reward Hacking Could Not Be Isolated

The original plan for this section was a mechanism analysis of WER-hacking (reduced pitch variance, compressed duration in `dpo_weronly`). The measured results (§5.3) make that analysis moot: **`dpo_weronly` does not hack.** It does not reach the lowest WER, and its naturalness drop (UTMOS 4.112 vs. `base` 4.309) is mild and undirected, not the signature of an intelligibility-for-naturalness trade. The cause is *optimization-induced, not reward-induced* — the opposite of the original framing: the shared DPO-FM objective is mis-specified (§6.7), so *both* reward definitions degrade, and the multi-objective arm (`dpo_multiobj`) collapses *more* than the WER-only arm, not less. Reward composition therefore cannot be isolated from the FM-collapse confound. We **retract** the claim that this work provides "the first reproducible reference case for WER-hacking in TTS"; what it provides is the more cautionary observation that, in a mis-specified preference-alignment framework, one cannot even reach the regime where reward hacking would manifest, and that adding SIM+UTMOS terms to the reward *worsens* rather than mitigates the outcome (the multi-objective best-vs-worst preference is the *cleanest* and hence the most off-manifold; §5.1, §6.7).

### 6.5 open-SOAR vs. External-Reward DPO: What the Decomposition Actually Shows (RQ4)

RQ4 asks how much robustness comes from reward-free self-correction (`open_soar`) vs. external multi-objective preference (`dpo_multiobj`). The measured answer is stark and one-directional: **all of the usable gain comes from the reward-free arm, and the external-reward arm subtracts rather than adds.** `open_soar` is the only treatment that improves over `base` (UTMOS 4.408 vs. 4.309; WER 0.093 vs. 0.103; SIM 0.844 vs. 0.891), whereas `dpo_multiobj` collapses on every axis (UTMOS 2.841, WER 0.752, SIM 0.365) and fails to generate at all on 25% of prompts. There is no "external multi-objective reward adds naturalness that self-correction cannot" story to tell — the external reward *destroys* naturalness here. This is consistent with the central diagnosis (§6.7) and its $n=100$ generalization (§5.1): `open_soar` trains toward a *real, on-manifold* clean target, while `dpo_multiobj` trains toward the *cleanest self-generated, off-manifold reward-winner*, which is exactly the most damaging target. The decomposition thus does **not** support combining the two; it identifies the reward-free, likelihood-aligned route as the only constructive one and the external-reward route as actively harmful on this model. (Figure 2's robustness-vs-naturalness scatter is retained only to *visualize* this separation, not to claim complementarity.)

### 6.7 An Automatic-Metric Reward Preference Anti-Correlates with the Flow Likelihood (Spearman $\rho=-0.187$, $N=188$): A Likelihood-vs-Quality Gap

The premise flagged in §3.2 — that an automatic-metric reward preference *agrees* with the flow-matching likelihood proxy $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ — is the single assumption on which the entire DPO-FM derivation rests. Here we test it directly, because throughout pilot training (§6.2) the reward margin stalled near $\sim$0.01 on most steps. That symptom admits three explanations, which a targeted probe can separate: **(H1, fixable noise)** the reward preference *does* agree with $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$, but a single $(\mathbf{x}_0,t)$ estimate is too high-variance, so Monte-Carlo averaging would recover the signal; **(H2, uninformative)** $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ reflects only reconstruction difficulty, uncorrelated with the automatic-metric reward; **(H3, anti-correlated)** the reward preference runs *against* $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ — the preferred candidate actually carries the *higher* $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$.

**Probe design (`alignment/probe_fm_reward.py`).** We run the probe over the **full set of $N=188$ `best_worst` pairs** and, for each, draw $K=16$ Monte-Carlo samples; within each draw the winner and loser share one $(\mathbf{x}_0,t)$ (the §3.2 invariant), and all forward passes run under `no_grad`. The measured quantity is the **denoised FM margin** $=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{\mathrm{lose}}-\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{\mathrm{win}}$ (positive $\Rightarrow$ the winner is the better fit, as DPO-FM assumes), MC-averaged over the $K$ draws, together with its correlation against the per-pair reward gap. Reproduce with `python alignment/probe_fm_reward.py --pairs pairs/out/pairs_best_worst.jsonl --n 188 --k 16`. We also retain the earlier $n=24$ pilot ($K=24$) as an explicit small-sample comparison.

**Result (full $N=188$): a robust monotonic anti-correlation; H1 refuted.** The load-bearing fact is a **weak but significant monotonic anti-correlation** between the denoised FM margin and the reward gap: **Spearman $\rho=-0.187$ ($p\approx10^{-2}$; $\rho^2\approx3.5\%$, weak by Cohen's convention; 95% CI $[-0.335,-0.036]$)**, with **Pearson $=-0.411$ ($p<10^{-7}$; $r^2=0.169$; 95% CI $[-0.556,-0.235]$) reported alongside as a leverage-amplified linear coefficient** — its 2.2× larger magnitude is inflated by a few high-leverage, large-reward-gap points. Leverage diagnostics (`alignment/probe_analysis.py`) confirm this is *amplification, not a few-point artifact*: trimming the top-5% $|$reward-gap$|$ pairs (10 pairs) still leaves Pearson $=-0.282$ (significant, same sign), and the maximum leave-one-out single-point influence on Pearson is only $0.038$ — no single pair drives the effect. With the critical value $|r|\approx0.143$ at $N=188$ (the 188 pairs come from 188 distinct prompts — one best-vs-worst pair each — so observations are prompt-level independent within this single-speaker corpus), both clear significance; we lead with the rank statistic because it is robust to those few high-leverage points. The larger a pair's reward advantage, the *more negative* its FM margin — the anti-correlation is sharpest on the largest-quality-gap pairs, exactly where it bites for DPO-FM. The complementary average is near-tie and *not* load-bearing: the margin is positive on **82/188 (44%)** of pairs (mean **$-0.0071$**), and the mean per-pair win-rate is **0.47** — a binomial test of 82/188 against 0.5 gives $p=0.093$ (not significantly different, **trending below 0.5**). We do **not** lean on this average as independent evidence: most of the 188 same-prompt pairs are near-ties (their candidates are acoustically too alike to separate), so a $\approx 0.47$ win-rate is largely a data-composition artifact; the diagnosis rests on the anti-correlation, whose survival on the high-gap (non-tie) subset we verify directly (`alignment/probe_analysis.py`, gap-stratified). Restricting to the largest-quality-gap pairs *strengthens* the effect rather than dissolving it: on the **top-50%** by $|$reward-gap$|$ ($\geq 1.390$, $n=94$) Spearman $\rho=-0.407$ (95% CI $[-0.585,-0.197]$; Pearson $-0.520$), and on the **top-25%** ($\geq 1.878$, $n=47$) Spearman $\rho=-0.440$ (95% CI $[-0.650,-0.160]$; Pearson $-0.477$) — both significant, versus $-0.187$ on the full $N=188$. The non-tie subset thus carries an anti-correlation **roughly twice as strong** as the pooled estimate, so the near-tie majority *dilutes* the effect rather than manufacturing it: precisely where a genuine quality difference exists — exactly where DPO-FM acts — the reward preference points *most* sharply against the model's own likelihood. The near-tie average is therefore a caveat about statistical power, not a sign the anti-correlation is an artifact of ties. This **refutes H1**: the single-draw margin standard deviation is **0.0892**, far larger than the $|$mean$|$ of $0.0071$, so individual steps are highly noisy — but the denoised signal does not concentrate at a positive (winner-preferring) value; it concentrates near $0$ on average while *systematically reversing* on the highest-quality-gap pairs. The flat $\sim$0.01 reward margin observed during DPO-FM training (§6.2) is therefore not a variance problem to be averaged away; it is the visible trace of a reward preference that, where it carries signal, points *against* the model's own likelihood.

**Self-correction over the $n=24$ pilot — an honest revision.** An earlier $n=24$ pilot ($K=24$) appeared markedly stronger: positive on only 4/24 (17%) of pairs, mean margin $-0.0215$, win-rate 0.33, Pearson $-0.496$, Spearman $-0.302$. Read literally, that pilot suggested the winner is *systematically* the worse FM fit. The full $N=188$ run **corrects this**: the average winner-dispreference was a **small-sample / high-leverage over-estimate** (win-rate $0.33\!\to\!0.47$, positive fraction $17\%\!\to\!44\%$, both regressing to chance), so we **retract** the pilot's "winner systematically dispreferred" reading. What *survived* scaling — and in fact strengthened — is the anti-correlation: Pearson held ($-0.496\!\to\!-0.411$) and Spearman, which was *not* significant at $n=24$ ($p\approx0.15$), became **significant** at $N=188$ ($p\approx10^{-2}$). We report this regression-to-chance explicitly because it is exactly the kind of small-sample artifact that an honest negative-result paper must surface rather than bury: the headline is the robust monotone anti-correlation (Spearman $\rho_s=-0.187$, $\rho_s^2\approx3.5\%$; Pearson $-0.411$ reported alongside as a leverage-amplified linear coefficient), **not** any claim that winners are on-average dispreferred.

**Mechanism (content typicality, not a specific knob).** $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ measures *typicality / likelihood under the model's own flow head*; the automatic-metric reward measures intelligibility and naturalness proxies (WER above all). On a best-vs-worst pair, the reward selects as winner whichever candidate scores highest — and that candidate tends to be the *more atypical (off-manifold)* member of the pair under the model's flow head, **regardless of which inference knob produced it**, so it carries the *higher* $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$. We deliberately state the mechanism at the level of *content typicality* rather than attributing it to guidance (or any single generation setting), because — as the confound analysis below establishes — the anti-correlation **survives partialling out the entire sampling grid** (partial $r=-0.392$ vs. raw $-0.411$): the off-manifold tendency is a property of *which member best-vs-worst selects*, not of a knob. This is the classic likelihood-vs-quality mismatch (Theis et al., 2016; Ho & Salimans, 2022), here domain-quantified on a continuous-latent TTS model as a weak but significant monotonic anti-correlation (Spearman $\rho=-0.187$, $\rho^2\approx3.5\%$, $N=188$; Pearson $-0.411$ alongside), sharpest on the largest-quality-gap pairs.

**Per-component decomposition: every perceptual axis points off-manifold, not just WER.** A natural worry is that the effect is merely "FM-vs-WER" — that the flow head dislikes only the intelligibility component. It is not. Correlating the denoised FM margin against each reward component's gap (`alignment/probe_analysis.py`) gives $\mathrm{corr}(\text{FM-margin},\text{WER-gap})=+0.412$, $\mathrm{corr}(\text{FM-margin},\text{SIM-gap})=-0.676$, and $\mathrm{corr}(\text{FM-margin},\text{UTMOS-gap})=-0.597$ (all significant). Re-expressed in the *reward-aligned* direction — lower WER, higher SIM, higher UTMOS are all "better" — all three axes anti-correlate with the FM margin: $-0.41$ for $(-\text{WER})$, $-0.68$ for SIM, and $-0.60$ for UTMOS. The "better" end of *each* perceptual axis is the more off-manifold one, and the speaker-similarity and naturalness axes anti-correlate *more strongly* than intelligibility. This widens the prescription from "fix the reward composition" to "**do not use $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ as a reward at all**": no reweighting of WER/SIM/UTMOS recovers a likelihood-aligned preference, because each component individually disagrees with the flow likelihood. One caveat tempers the strongest single number: on single-speaker LJSpeech the SIM metric is near-ceiling and weakly discriminative, so its $-0.68$ may be carried by the minority of pairs with a real SIM difference; the UTMOS $-0.60$ rests on a more discriminative metric and is the more robust of the three.

**It explains the entire tuning history of §6.2.** Under-fitting (margin $\to 0$) follows from the corpus being dominated by near-ties: on most same-prompt pairs the candidates are acoustically too alike to carry a separable signal, so the DPO-FM gradient has little to push on and the reward margin stalls near zero. Over-optimization (the degenerate escalation under a weak anchor) follows from the *anti-correlation*: on the minority of high-quality-gap pairs the only way to produce a positive margin is to oppose the FM head's own likelihood geometry, which — pursued without a strong anchor — globally distorts the flow field and collapses acoustic quality. The two opposite failure modes of §6.2 are thus two faces of the *same* reward/likelihood disagreement — no separable signal on the near-tie majority plus a wrong-way correlation on the high-gap minority (logged as B-15/B-16 in `docs/05`). The same mechanism yields a *falsifiable prediction* about downstream behaviour, which we state to be tested rather than assert as a result (magnitudes deferred to Tables 1–3): a reward-based DPO-FM arm should not merely under-fit but is expected to *actively degrade* relative to `base`, and the degradation should scale with the *purity* of the preference signal. Concretely, the cleaner and less tied a preference set — larger reward gaps, fewer near-ties — the more the wrong-way gradient dominates, so an arm built on the cleanest pairs is predicted to be harmed most, whereas a weak or heavily-tied signal should degrade only mildly. We pre-commit (before unblinding Tables 1–3) to the falsification rule: if the cleanest-pairs arm does **not** degrade more than `base` (CIs overlap), this prediction is wrong and we retract it rather than relabel it.

**Verdict (measured, $n=100$).** The prediction is **confirmed for `dpo_multiobj`**, the arm built on the cleanest (largest-gap, multi-objective best-vs-worst) preference signal: it *actively degrades* relative to `base`, with a WER CI of $[0.566, 1.035]$ that does **not overlap** `base`'s $[0.071, 0.142]$, plus a 25% immediate-EOS generation-failure rate (§5.1). Two honest qualifications attach. **First, the degradation is not attributable to the graded anti-correlation alone, but its precise character is unresolved.** The 25% no-payload failures are far more severe than a $\rho^2\approx3.5\%$ off-manifold pull would produce on its own; yet the available training curve **did not re-diverge** ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$ stable $\sim0.74$, margin $0.065$), so we report it conservatively as a **generation-time collapse on a prompt subset whose graded-vs-divergence status is mixed** (§5.1 caveat 2; manual curve + listening adjudication pending) rather than asserting an optimization divergence. The result thus confirms "active degradation" but **not** "active degradation purely as graded reward/likelihood disagreement." **Second, the purity-scaling sub-prediction is only directionally supported, not cleanly isolated.** Ordering by signal purity (cleanest `dpo_multiobj` → weak/heavily-tied `dpo_weronly`) does track outcome (catastrophic collapse → ≈ `base`), but the two arms differ in reward *composition*, not purity alone, so this is a confounded comparison; a clean purity test (cleanest-pairs vs. near-tie-pairs at a *fixed* reward definition) remains future work. We do not retract the prediction — the falsification condition (CIs overlap `base`) did not occur — but we report it at exactly this confidence.

**Confound resolved: the anti-correlation is intrinsic, not a generation-grid artifact.** A candidate-generation confound — winners systematically sampled at, say, higher guidance, so that a generation setting rather than content typicality drives both the reward and the $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ change — could in principle produce the negative $\rho$. We first ran a pure-metadata probe (`candidates/inspect_guidance_confound.py`) that joins all **188** preference pairs to their candidate generation settings (0 join failures). The winner/loser settings differ only weakly on every axis: **guidance scale** (winner mean $1.114$ vs. loser $1.093$, diff $+0.021$; winner higher in only 39% of pairs, lower in 29%, equal in 32%), **num-steps** (winner $11.85$ vs. loser $10.44$, diff $+1.42$; winner higher in 51%), and **speaker scale** (winner $1.371$ vs. loser $1.339$, diff $+0.032$); but that metadata probe measured the *wrong* quantity, $\mathrm{corr}(\text{reward-gap},\text{setting-gap})$ ($+0.108/+0.118/+0.108$). The diagnostically correct test, $\mathrm{corr}(\text{FM-margin},\text{setting-gap})$ (`alignment/probe_analysis.py`), gives the decisive picture: **num-steps (NFE) is a real but mild confounder** ($\mathrm{corr}(\text{FM-margin},\text{num-steps-gap})=-0.237$, 95% CI $[-0.360,-0.102]$, significant), while **guidance and speaker are not** ($-0.067$ each, n.s.). Higher-NFE candidates do carry a somewhat more negative FM margin — a genuine, if modest, grid effect. The verdict therefore turns on the **partial correlation** $\mathrm{corr}(\text{FM-margin},\text{reward-gap}\mid\text{guidance},\text{num-steps},\text{speaker})=\mathbf{-0.392}$ (95% CI $[-0.548,-0.209]$, significant). Controlling for the *entire* sampling grid — including num-steps, the one knob that is independently confounded — moves the linear coefficient only from $-0.411$ to $-0.392$, i.e., almost not at all: **the anti-correlation survives partialling**, and num-steps does not explain it. We therefore now state plainly that the effect is **intrinsic** to the preference–likelihood coupling and **not** an artifact of candidate generation. We are precise about what "intrinsic" means: the anti-correlation is *not a product of the generation knobs* (it survives controlling guidance, num-steps, and speaker over the full grid), **not** a claim that $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ is a bad likelihood proxy — it remains a serviceable one, which a likelihood-aligned preference (open-SOAR) optimizes to saturation. The lever is thus the preference *mechanism* — an automatic-metric reward selecting the off-manifold member of each pair — not the candidate sampler. (`alignment/probe_analysis.py` also reports a TOST equivalence test and a leverage/Cook's-distance diagnostic on the persisted per-pair data.)

**Which arms this implicates — revised by the $n=100$ results.** We had predicted that two arms escape the disagreeing premise: `open_soar` (reward-free, likelihood-aligned by construction) and `sft` (no win/lose $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ contrast). The measured results (§5.1) **confirm the first and refute the second**, and in doing so reveal a *broader* failure than the §6.7 contrast alone. **`open_soar` is confirmed unaffected and constructive**: its preference is defined by *trajectory cleanliness* (clean target vs. perturbed rollout, §3.4), so its training target is the *real, on-manifold* recording and it is the one arm that improves (best UTMOS, WER ≈ `base`). **`sft`, however, collapses** (WER 0.545, SIM 0.673, UTMOS 3.917) even though it performs no win/lose $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ comparison at all. The reward/likelihood anti-correlation of §6.7 therefore **cannot be the whole story**: `sft` does not inherit that contrast, yet it still degrades. The $n=100$-consistent reading is the broader thesis of §5.1 — **pulling the model toward its own automatic-reward-selected, off-manifold winners is harmful regardless of the objective (DPO *or* SFT)**; the §6.7 anti-correlation is the *DPO-specific instance* of a more general off-manifold-self-training pathology. The only escape that survives is training toward a genuinely on-manifold target (`open_soar`'s clean recording). We flag the confound from §5.1 (caveat 1): `sft`'s collapse is not cleanly separable from generic small-data SFT instability, and `open_soar`'s survival is only a *partial* control for it. Beyond `open_soar`, the diagnosis points to alignment mechanisms that neither rely on "$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$-as-logprob" nor self-train on reward-selected outputs: reward-weighted regression *anchored to real targets*, direct reward backpropagation through a differentiable vocoder, or a learned value/preference head — all left to future work.

**A training-side observation, now checked against held-out audio.** Running the identical DPO-FM optimizer on two preference signals that differ *only* in whether they align with the FM likelihood is *consistent with* the diagnosis. (1) **`dpo_multiobj`**, whose preference is the automatic-metric reward — which §6.7 shows anti-correlates with $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ — does not learn cleanly: the DPO loss sits near $\ln 2\approx0.69$ and the implied reward margin stays $\approx0.01$ on most steps. (2) **`open_soar`**, whose loser is a perturbed rollout that *intrinsically* incurs higher $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$, learns to saturation: DPO loss $0.12$–$0.22 \ll \ln 2$, reward-accuracy $1.0$, implied reward margin $\approx 4$–$8$. We are careful **not** to over-claim this as independent corroboration: open-SOAR's preference is aligned with the FM likelihood essentially *by construction* (a noised rollout has, near-definitionally, the larger velocity error and hence a large margin), so a saturating training curve is *expected*. **The decisive held-out evidence is now in (Table 1, §5.1, measured):** `open_soar`'s likelihood-aligned training is also the *only* arm that improves audio quality, whereas `dpo_multiobj`'s flat training curve in fact *understated* its true behaviour — on held-out audio it did not merely fail to learn but actively collapsed, generating no audio at all on 25% of prompts (a generation-time collapse on a prompt subset; whether graded or divergence is left open, §5.1 caveat 2). The training-dynamics contrast and the held-out outcome therefore agree in sign: the optimizer helps when, and only when, the preference is likelihood-aligned, and harms (sometimes catastrophically) when it is not. (We note `open_soar` here carries a $\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}=0.2$ clean-FM anchor; the RQ4 anchor-free variant reverts $\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}\!\to\!0.0$ and does not affect this contrast.)

## 7. Limitations

**Scale of the preference dataset.** The corpus comprises ~188 preference pairs — far smaller than production NLP RLHF (hundreds of thousands of pairs) and smaller than TTS efforts like Seed-TTS REINFORCE (millions of rollouts). We report deltas vs. `base`, make no absolute-SOTA claim, and cannot predict whether gains scale monotonically, saturate, or exhibit phase transitions. All effects carry bootstrap 95% CIs precisely because point estimates are unreliable at this scale.

**Single-speaker training distribution and weak SIM discriminability.** Reported preference-pair construction relies on LJSpeech, a single-speaker English corpus, so the SIM metric operates within a near-constant reference speaker and provides little gradient signal — partly by design (preventing SIM collapse) but meaning our evidence that multi-objective reward *actively preserves* speaker identity is weaker than in a multi-speaker or zero-shot setting. The released codebase implements a multilingual, multi-speaker protocol (VCTK, AISHELL-3, Mandarin seed-tts-eval) for scale-up, but the proportion of multi-speaker pairs realized here (__%) is insufficient for strong multi-speaker conclusions.

**Automatic-reward noise.** All three reward components are model-based, not human. ASR WER disagrees with human intelligibility on prosodically complex utterances; WavLM cosine imperfectly tracks perceived identity; UTMOS is trained on a narrow studio-speech distribution. We mitigate via per-prompt $z$-normalization but cannot remove the noise floor; pairs where all metrics disagree with human preference (estimated __%–__% from prior inter-rater agreement) may inject adversarial signal.

**Preference signal is bounded by the candidate quality gap (data-gap-limited).** DPO-FM treats the flow-matching velocity loss as an *implicit* reward, which can rank a pair only when its winner and loser carry a genuinely different acoustic quality. Our pilot training (§6.2) found the attainable reward margin to be upper-bounded not by the temperature $\beta$ or the anchor weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{sft}}$ but by the data itself: because winner and loser are sampled candidates for the *same* prompt, many pairs are near-ties whose candidates are acoustically too similar to separate, and on those pairs the DPO-FM loss collapses to its no-preference floor ($\ln 2$) regardless of optimization. The usable preference signal therefore concentrates in the minority of pairs with a real quality gap, making preference-pair *construction* — candidate-pool diversity (sampling-grid breadth and $K$) and an explicit reward-margin threshold that filters near-ties — an implicit ceiling on alignment, as load-bearing as the loss or $\beta$. We have not yet characterized the per-pair reward-gap distribution of the 188 pilot pairs; doing so, and rebuilding the corpus with a margin threshold (or a larger candidate pool) should near-ties dominate, is the immediate next step and remains unresolved in this work.

**An automatic-metric reward preference disagrees with the flow likelihood — the central caveat.** Beyond near-ties being unseparable (above), the more fundamental issue is that on dots.tts the win/lose $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ contrast — when the preference is an external automatic-metric reward — points the *wrong way* on exactly the pairs that carry a real quality gap. Measured directly on the **full $N=188$** preference set (§6.7), the denoised FM margin is **weakly but significantly monotonically anti-correlated** with the reward gap (Spearman $-0.187$, $p\approx10^{-2}$, $\rho^2\approx3.5\%$, weak by Cohen's convention; Pearson $-0.411$ reported alongside as a leverage-amplified coefficient), sharpest on the largest-gap pairs; on the near-tie majority the margin is uninformative (positive on 82/188, win-rate 0.47, binomial $p=0.093$ — trending below 0.5), which we treat as a data-composition artifact rather than independent evidence. We localize the failure carefully: it is **not** that $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ is a bad likelihood proxy — a likelihood-aligned preference (open-SOAR) optimizes it to saturation — but that an automatic-metric reward preference selects the more *atypical (off-manifold)* member of a pair, a textbook likelihood-vs-quality / classifier-free-guidance off-manifold gap (Theis et al., 2016; Ho & Salimans, 2022). The implication is that **likelihood-based DPO is the wrong tool** for this off-manifold preference, and the constructive routes are likelihood-aligned preferences (open-SOAR) or non-likelihood objectives (reward-weighted regression, direct reward backprop through a differentiable vocoder, a learned value head). Three honest caveats follow. First, this **reframes** rather than merely qualifies the contribution (Abstract/§1): the natural DPO-FM adaptation is the wrong tool here, which is why it is unstable, and we caution against transplanting discrete-token preference recipes to this architecture unexamined. Second, we explicitly **do not** claim that winners are *systematically* the worse FM fit on average: an earlier $n=24$ pilot suggested this (win-rate 0.33, positive on 4/24), but the full $N=188$ run shows that average effect **regressed toward chance** — we report the small-sample over-estimate transparently and rest the conclusion on the *anti-correlation*, which instead **strengthened** at scale (Spearman moved from non-significant at $n=24$ to significant at $N=188$). Third, the candidate-generation confound has now been **tested and excluded as the driver**. The diagnostically correct probe (`alignment/probe_analysis.py`) finds that, of the three sampling knobs, only num-steps/NFE is independently confounded with the FM margin ($\mathrm{corr}(\text{FM-margin},\text{num-steps-gap})=-0.237$, 95% CI $[-0.360,-0.102]$, significant; guidance and speaker $-0.067$, n.s.), yet the **partial correlation** $\mathrm{corr}(\text{FM-margin},\text{reward-gap}\mid\text{guidance},\text{num-steps},\text{speaker})=-0.392$ (95% CI $[-0.548,-0.209]$, significant) is essentially unchanged from the raw Pearson $-0.411$. The anti-correlation therefore **survives partialling out the entire grid** (num-steps included) and is **intrinsic** to the preference–likelihood coupling — where "intrinsic" means *not a generation-knob artifact*, **not** that $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ is a poor likelihood proxy (it is not; open-SOAR optimizes it to saturation). The diagnosis is unchanged: an automatic-metric reward selects the more off-manifold member of each pair — and a per-component check (§6.7) shows this holds for *all three* reward axes (reward-aligned anti-correlations $-0.41$ for lower-WER, $-0.68$ for SIM, $-0.60$ for UTMOS), so the prescription is to abandon $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$-as-reward rather than reweight it. This caveat was originally thought confined to the reward-based DPO-FM arms, but the held-out $n=100$ results (§5.1) show it is broader: `open_soar` (reward-free, likelihood-aligned, trained toward the *real* target) is the only surviving arm, whereas `sft` — which relies on no win/lose $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ contrast — *also collapses*. The operative caveat is therefore the broader off-manifold-self-training thesis of §5.1, with its own caveats (SFT-collapse/small-data confound, divergence-vs-graded, `LJ002` clustering) stated there.

**The $n=100$ collapse is directional, confounded, and non-uniform.** The headline reversal — `sft` and `dpo_multiobj` collapse while only `open_soar` improves — rests on a single-speaker LJSpeech held-out set with no human listening test, and the collapsed arms carry enormous CIs (`sft` WER $[0.251, 1.059]$). Three confounds block a clean mechanistic attribution and are first-class limitations: (i) the `sft` collapse cannot be separated from ordinary small-data SFT overfitting/forgetting without an `SFT-on-random-candidates` vs. `SFT-on-reward-winners` control (`open_soar`'s survival, at matched scale and optimizer family, is only a *partial* control); (ii) `dpo_multiobj`'s 25% immediate-EOS failures are far more severe than the *graded* off-manifold pull of §6.7 would produce, so the collapse magnitude is not attributable to the weak anti-correlation alone — yet the training curve did **not** re-diverge ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}^{w}$ stable $\sim0.74$, margin $0.065$), so whether this is graded degradation or optimization divergence stays **mixed/unresolved** (a generation-time collapse on a prompt subset; manual curve + listening adjudication pending); and (iii) the collapse concentrates in the `LJ002-xxxx` block (and *all* of `dpo_multiobj`'s generation failures fall there) — but that block is itself **89% of the eval set**, so the genuine, prompt-dependent signal is the within-block rate gap (`LJ001` 0/11 = 0% vs. `LJ002` 25/89 = 28%), only mildly length-linked (105 vs. 94 chars, $1.12\times$, no position bias) and not yet pinned to a single structural factor — so the pooled CIs mix a possibly-healthy region with a collapsed one. (The earlier $n=3$ smoke test that made `sft` look best, WER 0.039, sampled only the `LJ001` prefix and missed this entirely.) Until these are resolved, the broader "off-manifold self-training harms regardless of objective" thesis is a *cautionary hypothesis* supported by the data, not a controlled conclusion.

**ASR-to-expressiveness extrapolation.** WER-based reward implicitly targets intelligibility under a read-speech acoustic model. Whether the recipe generalizes to out-of-distribution expressive styles (theatrical, emotional, code-switching) is unvalidated; the gap between ASR performance and perceived naturalness may widen in high-expressiveness regimes underrepresented in the preference corpus.

**Wide confidence intervals from small held-out sets.** With 100 held-out utterances, bootstrap 95% CIs span roughly ±__ WER, ±__ SIM, ±__ UTMOS, so several fine-grained ablation differences (e.g., tuned vs. equal-weight) are not individually significant at $\alpha=0.05$. We report CIs rather than p-values alone and flag CI-crosses-zero findings as directional, not decisive.

**Scaling of continuous-latent DPO unexplored.** We do not study how DPO-FM behaves as (a) the backbone scales beyond Qwen2.5-1.5B, (b) the preference corpus grows, or (c) online/iterative DPO replaces offline. Whether the velocity-MSE proxy stays calibrated at larger scale, or whether the shared-$(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$ requirement bottlenecks high-throughput online sampling, are open questions. Whether alignment survives subsequent distillation (the natural next deployment step) is also left to future work.

## 8. Conclusion

We presented **DPO-FM**, an open preference-alignment adaptation for continuous-latent flow-matching AR TTS, instantiated on dots.tts — to our knowledge **among the first** for this architecture. The central technical move adapts Diffusion-DPO's heuristic — treat the flow-matching velocity-MSE loss as an implicit-likelihood proxy (which, under CFM, is an unbiased vector-field-MSE estimator rather than a proven NLL bound; Lipman et al., 2023) — enabling DPO directly over continuous AudioVAE latents with no discrete audio tokens; correctness hinges on sharing one $(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$ draw across all four forward passes, which we release as auditable Apache-2.0 code. Our central finding is cautionary and diagnostic, and we localize precisely what fails. A direct probe over the **full $N=188$ preference set** (§6.7) shows that on dots.tts an automatic-metric reward preference is **weakly but significantly monotonically anti-correlated** with the model's own flow likelihood (Spearman $-0.187$, $p\approx10^{-2}$, $\rho^2\approx3.5\%$; a leverage-amplified Pearson $-0.411$ alongside), sharpest on the largest-quality-gap pairs. The failure is **not** that $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ is a poor likelihood proxy — a likelihood-aligned preference (open-SOAR) optimizes it to saturation — but that the reward selects the more *atypical (off-manifold)* member of each pair: a textbook likelihood-vs-quality / classifier-free-guidance off-manifold gap (Theis et al., 2016; Ho & Salimans, 2022), here domain-quantified on a continuous-latent TTS model. The practical lesson is that **likelihood-based DPO is the wrong tool when the reward preference is off-manifold**; likelihood-aligned preferences (open-SOAR) or non-likelihood objectives (reward-weighted regression, direct reward backprop through a differentiable vocoder, a learned value head) are the right path. We are explicit that an earlier $n=24$ pilot over-stated an *average* winner-dispreference that regressed toward chance at full scale (82/188 positive, binomial $p=0.093$, trending below 0.5), while the anti-correlation persisted and gained significance — so the robust headline is the anti-correlation, not a claim that winners are systematically worse-fit. A confound analysis (`alignment/probe_analysis.py`) settles the intrinsic-vs-grid question: of the three sampling knobs only num-steps/NFE is independently confounded with the FM margin ($-0.237$, significant; guidance and speaker $-0.067$, n.s.), but the **partial correlation** $\mathrm{corr}(\text{FM-margin},\text{reward-gap}\mid\text{guidance},\text{num-steps},\text{speaker})=-0.392$ (95% CI $[-0.548,-0.209]$, significant) barely moves from the raw $-0.411$, so the anti-correlation **survives partialling out the entire grid** and is **intrinsic** to the preference–likelihood coupling — not a candidate-generation artifact, and (we are careful to add) not a sign that $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ is a poor likelihood proxy. A per-component check sharpens the lesson: the FM margin anti-correlates with the reward-aligned direction of *all three* components ($-0.41$ for lower-WER, $-0.68$ for SIM, $-0.60$ for UTMOS), so no reward reweighting rescues likelihood-alignment — the constructive conclusion is to abandon $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$-as-reward, not to retune it. A training-side observation is *consistent with* the diagnosis (the optimizer trains only when the preference is likelihood-aligned), but we flag it as largely **by construction** — open-SOAR's perturbed-rollout loser has a large FM margin near-definitionally — so the decisive evidence for any arm is held-out audio quality (Table 1, §5.1, **now measured**), not training dynamics — and that held-out evidence is summarized next. **The held-out reversal (measured, $n=100$).** The benchmark results sharpen this diagnosis into a broader, more cautionary lesson and overturn our own earlier reading. Of five arms, **only `open_soar` improves** (best UTMOS 4.408 vs. `base` 4.309, WER 0.093 ≈ `base` 0.103, modest SIM drop to 0.844); **`dpo_weronly` roughly holds**; **`sft` collapses** (WER 0.545, SIM 0.673, UTMOS 3.917); and **`dpo_multiobj` is a disaster** (WER 0.752, SIM 0.365, UTMOS 2.841 on the 75 prompts it could synthesize, plus a 25% immediate-EOS generation-failure rate). The damage is *monotone* in how hard each arm pulls the model toward its own automatic-reward-selected, off-manifold winners — and the one arm trained toward a *real, on-manifold* target (`open_soar`) is the only one that helps. This **refutes** the prior expectation — and a reviewer's designation of `sft` as the "truly unaffected control" — that SFT-on-winners is safe; it is not. The lesson generalizes beyond the DPO win/lose contrast: **self-training on automatic-reward-selected, off-manifold candidates degrades the model regardless of whether the objective is DPO or SFT**, and the constructive conclusion accordingly narrows from "open-SOAR *and* SFT are safe" to "**only open-SOAR is safe.**" The pre-registered *active-degradation* prediction (§6.7) is **confirmed** — `dpo_multiobj`, built on the cleanest preference signal, actively degrades vs. `base` with non-overlapping WER CIs ($[0.566,1.035]$ vs. $[0.071,0.142]$) plus 25% generation failures — though the failure is not purely the graded effect and its character — graded degradation vs. optimization divergence — is mixed and reported conservatively (the training curve did not re-diverge; §5.1 caveat 2), so it confirms "active degradation," not "active degradation purely as graded reward/likelihood disagreement." These collapse magnitudes are directional, not precise: single-speaker LJSpeech, no human listening test, enormous CIs on the failed arms, an `sft` collapse confounded with generic small-data SFT instability (`open_soar` is only a partial control), and all failures concentrated in the `LJ002-xxxx` block — which is itself 89% of the eval set, so the genuine signal is the within-block rate gap (`LJ001` 0% vs. `LJ002` 28%) rather than the block label (§7). open-SOAR is therefore the one direction we recommend pursuing. We further contribute **open-SOAR**, a reward-free open approximation of dots.tts's undisclosed SOAR mechanism that lets the community isolate self-correction from external reward — a decomposition previously impossible without proprietary code. The controlled reward-hacking comparison (WER-only vs. per-prompt $z$-normalized multi-objective reward) is reported above as a **negative result** — neither arm reaches the low-WER regime where hacking would manifest, so the reward-composition effect is confounded by FM collapse (Contribution 4 downgraded); a streaming-transfer test (1T1A, TTFA) remains the one forthcoming axis (Table 2, [TBD]; this run used `--skip-streaming`), with falsification rules pre-registered in §5.3 and §6.7. Given the limitations in §7 — small corpus, single-speaker distribution, automatic-reward proxies, single architecture — our primary contribution is an auditable adaptation together with a diagnosis of *when* its likelihood-based premise is the wrong tool — rather than an absolute performance claim — demonstrated on dots.tts and **possibly architecture-specific** (pending replication on, e.g., VoxCPM). We release all code, data scripts, and the diagnostic probe to enable others to test, and improve on, the alignment of continuous-latent TTS.

## Ethics Statement

**Voice cloning and impersonation.** Improving naturalness and speaker fidelity lowers the effort to produce convincing impersonations; the techniques here could be misused for non-consensual voice clones, fraud, or circumventing speaker verification. We do not release pre-trained aligned weights optimized for zero-shot cloning of arbitrary speakers.

**Training-data consent.** All datasets are publicly released corpora with documented consent for research use; we use no scraped or undocumented audio. License details are in `data/LICENSES.md`. Practitioners extending this recipe to proprietary or crowd-sourced audio should independently verify consent and comply with data-protection law.

**Watermarking and provenance.** The released code omits an audio watermarking module, which we treat as a limitation. We recommend downstream users apply a perceptual watermark (e.g., SilentCipher, AudioSeal) for provenance, and flag verifying alignment–watermark compatibility as future work.

**Evaluation bias.** Whisper WER, WavLM SIM, and UTMOS are trained primarily on standard English/Mandarin studio speech; their behavior on accented, dialectal, or highly expressive speech may be biased, potentially steering optimization toward "standard" speech that disadvantages underrepresented communities. Scores for out-of-distribution speakers should be treated with caution.

**Scope of claims.** This is a research artifact documenting methodology and controlled experiments, not a deployment-ready system; no warranty is made for sensitive or high-stakes applications.

## Reproducibility Statement

**Code and recipe.** The full pipeline — DPO-FM loss (`alignment/losses_dpo.py`), the open-SOAR perturbed-rollout generator, the multi-objective reward scorer, preference-pair construction, and all evaluation harnesses — is released under Apache-2.0 (URL upon acceptance). End-to-end reproduction is documented in `docs/03-REPRODUCE.md` with pinned versions (`requirements.txt`), dataset download/preprocessing commands, and expected intermediate outputs.

**The shared-$(\mathbf{x}_0, t)$ requirement.** Correctness depends on the four passes consuming identical noise/timestep samples, enforced via `torch.random.fork_rng()` with a fixed seed and verified by a unit test (`tests/test_shared_rng.py`) that runs on a single CPU in under a minute, so reviewers can validate the derivation without training.

**Compute.** Preference-pair generation (K = 4–8 rollouts/prompt) runs on 2×H100 (80 GB) in ~__ GPU-hours; DPO-FM fine-tuning for $600$ steps takes ~__ GPU-hours on the same hardware (final wall-clock to be logged at the production run). Full-parameter fine-tuning is feasible on a single A6000 (48 GB) with gradient checkpointing and reduced batch size; a LoRA variant (`alignment/lora_config.py`) is provided. All evaluation scripts run on CPU or a single GPU with documented wall-clock times.

**Non-reproducible baseline.** The official `dots.tts-soar` weights are an upper-bound reference only (training procedure not public); we label this row in all tables. Our `open_soar` is a documented approximation, not an exact replication.

**Run journal.** `docs/RUN_JOURNAL.md` records the chronological run sequence, hyperparameters, metric trajectories, and protocol deviations as a first-person audit trail, so readers can separate reported results from exploratory runs.
